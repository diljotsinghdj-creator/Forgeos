# Visual language: what goes on screen

The material a film is made of: footage, archive, maps, documents, screenshots, illustration, typography, and how
they combine. Read this when setting the film's DNA and when filling scene cards. visual_decision_engine.md decides
*which* of these a line gets; this file says how to use each one well.

Labels: [V] source-backed (sources.md P#, case_studies/, PATTERNS.md) · [I] interpretation · [O] our rule.

**Evidence note.** The research could not view frames (sources.md, case-study evidence_basis). The [V] statements
below come from interviews, descriptions and write-ups, not from frame analysis. The shot-level rules are [O]: our
craft standard, to be checked against frames when access allows (learning_protocol.md).

---

## 1. What the studied craft shows

- [V] **The visual mode follows the claim type:** maps for space and power, location footage for lived experience,
  archive and witnesses for buried history (PATTERNS: history/geography §3, politics §3).
- [V] **The layout mixes modes:** maps with animation on top, news clips, photos and data visualisation (P22).
- [V] **Real material over illustration when it exists:** the black-hole piece stresses that the image is assembled
  real data, not an artist's impression (black-hole-photo-big-deal-2019).
- [V] **Footage sources are credited with timestamps** in descriptions (from-spy-to-president-putin-2017).
- [V] **Handmade materials** (construction paper) for some films: a schooling film evoked a child's craft project,
  e.g. a paper clock that turns into a pie chart (P8).
- [V] **Clip or photo is an editorial choice:** a clip gives context, a photo can carry an abstract idea, and the
  framing of a photo can flatter or not, so it is chosen for neutrality (P15).
- [V] **Visual evidence is the built-in priority** of an editorial video: what can be shown that proves the story
  (P16).
- [V] **A shared style guide with room for each producer's voice** (P25).
- [V] **The artefact is the evidence** in visual-history pieces: two photographs compared (famous-war-photo).
- [V] **Hand-built datasets become visuals:** every lyric mention logged and sorted by year (why-rappers-love-grey-poupon-2016).

## 2. The material, mode by mode [O]

| Material | Use it to | Treatment | Watch out |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Documentary / location footage** | Put the viewer in the place; show human consequence | Hold longer (4–7 s); natural sound up; minimal graphics on top | Must be the real place, or badged ILLUSTRATIVE |
| **News footage** | Date-stamp a real event | Source and date lower-left; consistent grade so clips from different broadcasters sit together | Logos of broadcasters: crop or credit, never as decoration |
| **Stock footage** | Atmosphere and connective tissue only | ≤ 25% of runtime; never for a specific place, person or event without ILLUSTRATIVE | The swap test: generic stock fails it |
| **Archival photographs** | Prove the past; give a face to an era | A slow push to the detail named; optional parallax; consistent archive grade; credit | Never retouch evidence; no colourisation presented as original |
| **Historical footage** | Motion from the past | Speed-correct; letterbox or frame it as an artefact; credit | Label dates; don't mix eras without saying so |
| **Maps** | Place, route, border, spread | vox_maps_and_geography.md | Projection honesty, disputed lines |
| **Satellite imagery** | Physical change; places we can't film | Before/after wipes; dates labelled; source line (NASA, ESA Sentinel, USGS) | Resolution limits: don't claim what the pixels can't show |
| **Newspapers** | Public record of a moment; how it was reported | Shown as an object (paper texture, edges), headline highlighted on the word, pushed in | Read the article: the headline may mislead |
| **Documents** | The primary source as the star | Held still to be read; the exact line highlighted (signal colour) as it is read; redactions kept | Never alter; crop to the relevant passage with the source line |
| **Screenshots** | Platforms, posts, official pages | Clean frame, the relevant element highlighted, date and URL shown | Private individuals' names and faces blurred |
| **Interface recreations** | Show how a product behaves when screenshots can't | Pixel-true but simplified; badged RECREATION | Official logos only when the narration names the platform |
| **Illustrated scenes** | Things no camera recorded; hypotheticals; psychology | One drawing language per film; badged ILLUSTRATIVE when depicting real events | No cartoons over real faces |
| **Animated objects** | Make a concept physical (a coin, a pill, a container) | Built from the subject's own material; behaves by real physics | One object metaphor per idea |
| **Visual metaphors** | Abstract ideas | Introduced and labelled as a metaphor; never the last film's | Don't let the metaphor make a claim the evidence doesn't |
| **Split screens** | Compare, contrast, before/after | Equal halves, same scale, labelled | Not more than 2–3 panels |
| **Graphic compositions / collage** | Combine evidence, era and idea | Layered cut-outs over texture, each piece sourced | A collage is not evidence; keep the sources separate |
| **On-screen labels** | Name what the viewer sees | Short (1–4 words), near the subject, on the word | Never label what is obvious |
| **Typography** | Hierarchy, voice, emphasis | §3 | One display face and one text face per channel |

## 3. Typography [O]

- **The channel layer is fixed:** two typefaces (display and text), caption style and source-line style
  (MASTER_DIRECTIVES 20/80).
- **The film layer varies:** the treatment (weight, case, tracking, texture, colour role) taken from the subject:
  typewriter-ish for archives, tabular figures for data, engraved for institutions, a clean UI face for technology.
- **Hierarchy:** headline (key phrase, 1–5 words) › label › source line. Never more than three levels in one frame.
- **Highlight, don't underline everything:** the one key word or number in the signal colour (#FFDD33).
- **Legibility:** text at least ~3.5% of frame height for labels and 6% or more for key numbers; contrast checked on
  a phone.
- **Text on screen ≠ narration on screen.** Show the key fact; let the voice say the sentence.

## 4. Composition and screen layout [O]

- **One focal point per frame.** Everything else is quieter (lower contrast, smaller, desaturated).
- **Safe zones:** keep labels inside the 90% title-safe area and away from YouTube's progress bar and chapter overlay
  (bottom ~12%) and the end-screen area in the last 20 s.
- **Consistent anchors:** the source line always in the same corner; the badge always in the same corner; chapter
  titles always enter the same way.
- **Layering order** (motion_graphics §5): texture → base → data → annotation → labels → badge and source.
- **Colour roles:** base (the ground), accent (the film's own colour: "the other side", the second series), signal
  (#FFDD33: "look here", key numbers). Evidence keeps its own colours, with a consistent archive grade.

## 5. Building a film's look from its subject [O]

1. List the native material (visual_decision_engine.md §2).
2. Sample three to five hues from that material; assign base, accent and supports; the signal is fixed.
3. Choose texture from the material's surface (paper, metal, glass, soil, screen).
4. Choose the type treatment from how the subject writes (ledgers, signage, code, letters).
5. Write three key frames in words and do the swap test against the last three films.

## 6. Failure modes

| Failure | Fix |
|---|---|
| Stock montage under explanation | Replace with the native material or a graphic that explains |
| A desk globe, a pin map or a counter standing in for the subject | Draw the subject itself (BRAIN_LOG 2026-11-11) |
| Every frame the same paper texture and black title box | Look from the subject, rotated by history (BRAIN_LOG 2026-11-11) |
| Evidence covered by graphics | Annotation beside, not over; let it be read |
| An unlabelled recreation | RECREATION / ILLUSTRATIVE badge |

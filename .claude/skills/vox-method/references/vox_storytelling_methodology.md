## 1. What the studied craft shows

- [V] **Research front-loaded, story never skimped** (P12, P13).
- [V] **A two-column script:** voiceover on the left, visuals on the right, written together (P13). Another former
  producer's process: pitch → research document → outline → script, with the visual idea at the core (P14).
- [V] **The visual column is filled with the real thing:** the planned archive item, or a pasted placeholder frame for
  a shot still to get. A long run of voiceover against a single visual cell means the visual homework is not done.
  All assets are gathered, and map tests made, before a word is recorded (P13).
- [V] **Write to a found document:** a historical article or record is found during research and the script is
  written specifically to it (P16).
- [V] **One visual hook, built up:** a producer adopted the diagram her experts kept returning to (the circle of
  fifths) and built every later concept on it (P16).
- [V] **A three-part shape with bookends** (one former producer, on his own channel): teaser; part one gives context
  and any simple answer early; part two is the substance; part three is what it means; a conclusion echoes the
  teaser (P13).
- [V] **One memorable moment beats total coverage:** a long-form series aimed for a moment per episode that sticks,
  rather than jamming in every fact (P18).
- [V] **The delayed reveal:** the black-hole video explains why imaging one is hard *before* showing the photo
  (P3, black-hole-photo-big-deal-2019).
- [V] **Format devices organise chapters,** e.g. "explained in N moments", with a recurring visual device per moment
  (P15).
- [V] **Openings reset an assumption** with a number, a date or an official claim, then ask why. Seen in all four
  category syntheses (PATTERNS §1 of each).
- [V] **Hands-on test before theory:** run the algorithm, compare the two photographs (are-we-automating-racism-2021,
  famous-war-photo-staged-errol-morris-2020).
- [V] **A borrowed analogy world or a proxy person** carries a mechanism: fictional houses for trading nations, a
  cartoon character as the median worker (trade-wars-explained-2018, homer-simpson-economic-analysis-2016).
- [V] **Endings vary with the question:** a direct verdict, an open question about distribution, remedies matched to
  causes, a reframe ("should it exist at all?"), or a settled fact with an unknown motive (econ/tech §2, sci/culture
  §2).
- [V] **Length follows the subject:** single-snapshot disputes at about 5 minutes, origin-plus-present stories at about
  14–16, long multi-segment pieces at about 22 (politics §7, sci/culture §2).

- [V] **Measured on recent explainers (case_studies/TRANSCRIPT_STUDY.md and the four watch-based case studies):**
  - the central question lands at a median of about **1:00**, after a concrete cold open (range 0:27–2:05);
  - arguments by causes are numbered aloud ("the first reason…", "the second way…") in three of six transcripts;
  - **five of six endings return to the opening** (the refrain, the company, the chant, the sharks);
  - sponsor or membership reads sit in the middle third or at the very end;
  - a chart that returns three times becomes a deliberate anchor.

## 2. The two-column script [O, after P13/P14]

Write the visual and the words together, beat by beat. Our format uses the CreatorForge beat order:

```
[VISUAL / MOTION CUE] satellite pass over the lock flight; camera pushes to the top chamber
[AUDIO / SFX]         low wind; one metallic clank as the gate closes
[NARRATOR]            This ship is about to climb a hill.            (c4)
```

- The visual is written first, because it decides what the words don't have to say.
- Each beat holds one idea. If you need "and", it's probably two beats.
- Every fact carries its dossier claim id in brackets.

## 3. Hooks (first 5–30 seconds) [O, with [V] patterns]

The hook must land inside 5 seconds and hold to 30. It needs a first frame, a first line and a reason to stay. Choose
the mechanism for *this* subject, and never the previous film's.

| Mechanism | How it works | Fits | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Assumption reset** | State what everyone believes; break it with one fact | contested history, myths, prices | [V] PATTERNS §1 (all) |
| **Hard number** | One startling figure with a body | inequality, scale, cost | [V] divided-island, gerrymandering |
| **Says vs does** | The official claim, then the record | institutions, states, companies | [V] China islands, Syria |
| **Live test** | Run the experiment in front of the viewer | technology, psychology, perception | [V] automating-racism |
| **Two-document diff** | Two near-identical images or documents; what changed? | forensic, history, photography | [V] war-photo |
| **Delayed object** | Show the thing's importance before the thing | science results, unveilings | [V] black-hole |
| **Ordinary object, huge system** | A mundane object hides a machine | economics, logistics, culture | [V] Grey Poupon, diapers |
| **Micro-moment** | One hyper-specific moment, place and time, then zoom out | investigations, human stories | [O] MASTER_DIRECTIVES |
| **Aftermath first** | The wreckage, then rewind | disasters, scandals | [O] Reverse Autopsy engine |

Hook rules [O]:
- The first frame is the subject, never a title card or logo.
- State the central question explicitly by about 1:00 (measured median on recent explainers; range 0:27–2:05), after a
  concrete cold open has earned it. Under 0:30 only when the hook *is* the question (TRANSCRIPT_STUDY.md).
- Promise what the film will deliver (the "by the end you'll know…" contract), in our own words.
- Pay off the title and thumbnail promise within the first minute, at least partly.

## 4. Narrative architectures [O, mapped to [V] structures]

Choose by the kind of claim. CreatorForge engines (Reverse Autopsy, Live Viewer Trap, Trojan Horse, Historical Echo)
are hook-and-arc packages that sit on top of these.

| Architecture | Beats | Use for |
|---|---|---|
| **Question ladder** | hook → question → context → step 1 → step 2 → turn → answer → implication | most explainers |
| **Cast, then twist** | introduce actors one by one → reveal hidden alignments | many-actor conflicts (Syria) |
| **Chronology with hinges** | dated turning points, each changing the situation | origins, "how did we get here" |
| **Problem, proof, fix** | mechanism → worked case → alternative elsewhere | policy, institutions |
| **Controlled comparison** | hold one thing constant, find the variable | gaps between neighbours |
| **Forensic** | provocation → evidence → dead end → decisive clue → verdict with residual doubt | "was it real?" |
| **Competing explanations** | line up theories, test each on the same object | economic debates |
| **Lineage → breakthrough** | history of attempts → what changed → how it works → stakes | technology, science |
| **Object biography** | one object travels through time and carries the story | culture, history |
| **Escalation → reframe** | everyday case → mechanism → high-stakes case → the bigger question | society, ethics |
| **Road / route** | stops along a real route each illustrate a part | geography, place stories |

## 5. Chapters and open loops [O]

- 3–6 chapters for 8–15 minutes. Each has a **job** (what the viewer understands after it) and an **on-screen title**
  of two to five words.
- Each chapter ends on an **open loop**: a question, an anomaly, or a "but". The next chapter opens by picking it up.
- The **turn** (the moment the answer changes shape) sits around 60–75% of runtime.
- Recurring device: a signature visual that returns at each chapter turn (visual_decision_engine.md §2.8).

## 6. Transitions in the script [O]

Write the bridge into the words as well as the picture:
- **Question bridge:** end a chapter on the question the next answers.
- **"But" turn:** "That explains the price. It doesn't explain why it kept rising."
- **Time hinge:** a date as the first word of a new beat (chronologies).
- **Object return:** the carrying object reappears changed (the diaper, now a chart bar).
- **Scale shift:** "Zoom out, and the same thing is happening in…"

## 7. Explanatory moves [O, with [V] where noted]

- **Analogy world** [V trade wars]: map an unfamiliar system onto a familiar one; flag where the analogy breaks.
- **Proxy person** [V Homer]: a representative case stands in for a statistic; we use a composite or a documented
  case, labelled.
- **Baseline and counter-signal** [V future of work]: show what normal looks like before the anomaly.
- **Unit equivalence** [V clean energy]: convert to a familiar unit, verified and sourced.
- **Worked example:** run the mechanism once, slowly, with real numbers, then show it at scale.
- **The anticipated objection:** voice the viewer's doubt ("Couldn't they just…?") and answer it.
- **Show, then name:** show the phenomenon, then give its name, not the reverse.

## 8. Emotional pacing [O]

- Alternate density: explanation runs (fast, building graphics) and evidence/human beats (slower, held, with ambience).
- One emotional peak per act at most. Earn it with specifics, not music alone.
- After a reveal or a human consequence, leave a beat of silence (0.5–1.5 s) before the next line.
- Long films need breathers (P18): a hold on a place, a sound, a document, with no narration.

## 9. Endings [O, with [V] variety]

Pick the ending the question deserves; never a generic "like and subscribe".
- **Verdict:** answer the question plainly, with the confidence the evidence allows.
- **Remedy:** what would change the outcome, matched to the causes found.
- **Open question:** the unresolved part, stated honestly.
- **Reframe:** the bigger question the film has earned.
- **Return:** the opening object or moment, seen differently now.
- The final line lands on picture: the last frame is evidence or the signature device, not a logo.

## 10. Script format and audit

- Write `script.md` chapter by chapter in the beat format of §2.
- Estimate runtime at about 2.8 words/second (measured median of recent Vox explainers); 8–15 minutes ≈ 1,350–2,500 words.
- Run CreatorForge's checks (banned phrases, pattern interrupt every 20 s) and the blueprint validator once scenes
  exist.
- End with the **retention and mistake audit**: the weakest 10% rewritten, any 20-second stretch without a new kind of
  picture fixed, a fact audit (every figure has a claim id), and a balance audit for contested topics.

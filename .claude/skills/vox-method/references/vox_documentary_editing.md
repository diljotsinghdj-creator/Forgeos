# Documentary editing: shot selection, rhythm, cutting, continuity, grade

How the film is assembled so that it reads at speaking pace and never goes slack. Read this when timing the blueprint
and when cutting. The pacing caps here are CreatorForge's gate 4.

Labels: [V] source-backed (sources.md P#, case_studies/) · [I] interpretation · [O] our rule.

**Evidence note.** The research could not view frames, and no source described Vox's cutting patterns or grading in
detail (sources.md §I gaps). The [V] base is thin. The rules below are our craft standard [O], labelled as such, to be
tested against frames when access allows.

## 1. What the studied craft shows

- [V] After Effects for animation (one producer assembles whole sequences there), Premiere for the final edit and
  most sound work (P13, P27, P30).
- [V] Visual changes cut on the music's beat in at least one studied video (P3).
- [V] A producer owns the whole film, including the edit (P1, P24 snippets; confirmed by P6, P9, P42): one senior
  producer pitches, researches, scripts with a story editor, then voices, animates and cuts (P9).
- [V] Footage shot on location is planned with previs: map moves used to plan drone flights (P23). Location shoots
  were short and intense: about 11 days per place, with pre-production done at home (P17). In the first season of a
  field series one reporter travelled for about three months to make six films, posting dispatches on the way (P31).
- [V] Transitions carry no information, so the default is a jump cut; a transition is used rarely, for pacing (P16).
- [V] J- and L-cuts (sound before picture) and cutting on action lift a self-shot sequence (P13). A third-party
  breakdown shows one "invisible" sequence change: a short 3D camera push across the edit with a brief blur peaking on
  the cut (P28).
- [V] One producer reports 72–75% average retention whether a film runs six or twelve minutes (P9).
- [V] Watch time grew to half of viewers watching at least 4 minutes (P35). [I] The editing is built to hold attention
  through the middle of the film, not just the hook.

## 2. Shot selection [O]

For every line, the editor asks three questions in order:
1. **Is there real material for this exact line?** Use it (evidence first).
2. **If not, which graphic explains it?** (visual_decision_engine.md.)
3. **What did the previous shot use?** Change the kind of picture at least every 20 seconds, and don't put two shots
   of the same mode back to back without a reason.

Prefer the specific over the general, the primary over the secondary, the moving over the still (when the motion
means something), and the quiet frame over the busy one.

## 3. Framing and composition [O]

- Evidence frames: the subject large, readable, centred or on the thirds; nothing covering it.
- Graphics: one focal point; the camera move points at it.
- Footage reframing: punch in to remove irrelevant edges (only on 4K or better sources), never to change meaning.
- Keep text and key subjects out of the bottom ~12% (the YouTube controls) and out of the end-screen zone.

## 4. Rhythm and pacing [O + CreatorForge gate 4]

| Rule | Value |
|---|---|
| Still shot (one picture held) | ≤ 4.5 s |
| Building shot (assembles on screen) | ≤ 7 s |
| Last shot of the film | +1.5 s allowed |
| Average hold across the film | ≤ 5 s |
| New *kind* of picture | at least every 20 s |
| Same effect | ≤ 3 times per film |

- **Vary the hold.** Constant 4-second cuts become a metronome. Mix 1.5–2.5 s punches (lists, montage), 3–5 s
  explanations and 5–7 s builds.
- **Cut on the word:** a cut lands on the stressed syllable of the word that introduces the new idea, or just before.
- **Cut on the beat** where music leads (P3). Use the beat grid as a guide, not a cage.
- **Breathing holds:** after a reveal or a human consequence, hold 0.5–1.5 s past the line.
- **Acceleration and release:** speed up through escalation, release at the turn, slow for the payoff.

## 5. Cutting patterns [O]

| Pattern | Use | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Straight cut | The default for a new idea | Needs a cause: a new idea, a turn, a time jump |
| Match cut | "This is the same as that" | Shape, motion or colour matched across the cut |
| J-cut (sound leads) | Enter a place or chapter | Ambience of the next scene 0.5–1 s before the picture |
| L-cut (sound trails) | Let a moment linger | The last scene's sound under the new picture |
| Smash cut | Contradiction ("They said X. [cut] Y.") | Use sparingly: at most twice a film |
| Montage | Many examples showing one pattern | 1–2 s each, one rhythm, one unifying frame or label |
| Hold to black | The heaviest beat | Once a film, at most |

No two plain cuts in a row without a cause. Rotate transition families across films (motion_graphics §7).

## 6. Scene transitions and continuity [O]

- **Continuity of place:** after any cut to a close view, the viewer still knows where they are (a locator inset, the
  same base map, a returning establishing shot).
- **Continuity of object:** the carrying object (the pill, the container) keeps its look throughout.
- **Continuity of colour:** palette roles fixed across the film; footage graded toward the palette's base temperature.
- **Continuity of type:** the same label style and source-line position everywhere.

## 7. Footage treatment and grade [O]

- One **archive grade** per film (e.g. lifted blacks, slight warmth or cool) applied to all archival material so it
  sits together. It is part of the film's DNA, not always sepia.
- News footage: normalise exposure and saturation; don't stylise evidence until it looks fake.
- Never alter what evidence shows. Grading changes tone, never content. Every modification goes in the asset
  register.
- Frame-rate handling: conform everything to the project rate; a deliberate stepped rate for cut-out animation is a
  style choice (motion_graphics §6).

## 8. The edit sequence [O]

1. **Radio cut:** narration only, timed from real audio, with pauses placed.
2. **Blueprint assembly:** drop every scene at its in/out from the blueprint's edit list.
3. **Evidence pass:** real material placed and checked against the asset register.
4. **Graphics pass:** animatics, then finals, synced to words.
5. **Sound pass:** music cues, SFX, ambience, silence (vox_sound_design.md).
6. **Pacing pass:** check holds against caps; find any 20 s stretch without a new kind of picture.
7. **Watch-through at speed** on a phone, with sound off once (the mute test) and once with sound only.
8. **QC** (documentary_quality_control.md).

## 9. Failure modes

| Failure | Fix |
|---|---|
| One sentence per shot, all held 5–10 s | Split lines; vary hold lengths (BRAIN_LOG 2026-11-11) |
| A metronome edit | Mix punches, explanations, builds and breaths |
| Transitions as decoration | A cut, or a family that says the relationship |
| Lost in place after a cut | Locator inset or continuous camera |
| Mismatched archive looks | One archive grade |

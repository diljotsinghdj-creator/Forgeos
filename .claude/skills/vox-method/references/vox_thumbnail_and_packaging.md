# Thumbnails, titles and YouTube packaging

The promise the film makes before anyone presses play, and how the opening keeps it. Read this at pitch time (a film
whose title and thumbnail can't be written is not ready) and again before publishing.

Labels: [V] source-backed (sources.md P#, case_studies/, PATTERNS.md) · [I] interpretation · [O] our rule.

---

## 1. What the studied craft shows

- [V] **Titles are the question or the promise:** "Why…", "How…", "Who is fighting and why", "…explained",
  "The real reason…", "Was this… staged?" (PATTERNS: all four sets, §7/§8).
- [V] **Scope promises:** "in 2 minutes", "a brief, simple history". Risk: "simple" on a contested topic invites
  audits (israel-palestine-brief-simple-history).
- [V] **The deictic "this":** "this black hole photo", "this famous war photo", "this instrument". The title points at
  the thumbnail object and leaves the identifying to the image (PATTERNS sci/culture §7).
- [V] **Flat startling declaratives:** "When white supremacists overthrew a government"
  (when-white-supremacists-overthrew-a-government).
- [V] **"feat." credit** when a known investigator stars (famous-war-photo).
- [V] **Thumbnails with a single central subject and one text element** (P36, one example cited by a third party).
- [I] **Impact-framed titles** rather than bare topics (P37: one outside creator's opinion; its example titles are
  illustrative, not checked Vox titles).
- [V] **Test the package before greenlighting:** one former producer makes the thumbnail and headline first and
  shows them to a private group of creators before committing to the film (P12).
- [V] **Series brands carry the method** (Borders: travel along a line; Atlas: maps; Missing Chapter: buried history)
  (PATTERNS history/geography §7).
- [V] **Companion packages** (article, interactive, playlist, resource list) and **timestamped source lists** in
  descriptions (PATTERNS politics §6, sci/culture §7).
- [V] **Re-cuts and Shorts** extend a film (Syria re-made; Nevada cut into a Short) (PATTERNS history/geography §7).
- [V] Strategy: the highest average views per video among news publishers, about 2 million in a January 2025
  ranking (P42 citing Press Gazette; P2, P34 snippet only); watch time grew to half of
  viewers watching at least 4 minutes (P35); YouTube-first formats, metadata and a consistent identity credited for
  growth (P24).

- [V] **Length:** Vox's video director calls the 5–15 minute "medium form" the one the outlet has perfected
  (P2, 2022). The channel sample measures a median of 9.1 minutes for recent standalone explainers, with the
  middle half between 7.3 and 11.6 minutes (case_studies/CHANNEL_SAMPLE.md).

## 2. Titles [O]

- **Formula options:** the question ("Why does X…?"), the promise ("How X really works"), the reveal ("The real reason
  X"), the deictic object ("Why this X explains Y"), the declarative shock ("When X did Y"), the forensic yes/no
  ("Was X faked?").
- **Rules:** under ~60 characters; the most specific noun first; no clickbait the film can't pay off; no ALL CAPS;
  no emoji. The title and thumbnail say different things that add up.
- **Write five titles at pitch time,** pick one, keep two for A/B testing if available.
- **Never** imply a finding the dossier doesn't support.

## 3. Thumbnails [O]

- **One subject, big:** the native object or evidence (a document, a place, a chart's key mark), recognisable at
  phone size (test at 160×90 px).
- **At most 3–4 words of text,** and not the title repeated.
- **One signal element** in #FFDD33 (CreatorForge): the circled detail, the key number, the arrow.
- **Faceless:** no presenter. Real people only via real, credited photos used fairly, never AI faces.
- **Contrast between films:** the thumbnail's composition and dominant hue change film to film (the film palette).
- **Honest:** the thumbnail's object appears in the film, ideally in the first minute.
- CreatorForge: thumbnails are images, so ask the owner before making them.

## 4. Opening retention [O]

- The first frame is the thumbnail's promise, in motion, within 5 seconds.
- The central question is stated by about 0:30.
- No intro logo, no "in this video", no channel plug before the hook (MASTER_DIRECTIVES 20/80).
- Pay off part of the promise within the first minute; keep the rest as an open loop.

## 5. Description, chapters, end screens [O]

**Description template:**
```
<One or two sentences: the question and why it matters. Not a teaser that lies.>

Sources and further reading:
0:00 <source> · <link>
...
Data and documents we built: <companion link, if any>

Chapters:
0:00 <hook chapter title>
...
<Corrections: (only if needed)>
```
- **Chapters:** the first at 0:00; at least three; titles are the chapter titles from the blueprint (2–5 words, the
  curiosity not the answer).
- **End screen:** the last 10–20 s; keep the frame's lower and right areas clear of key content; point to the film
  that answers the next question.
- **Pinned comment:** a sourcing note or a question that invites informed discussion, never "smash like".

## 6. Length [O, with [V] support]

Length follows the subject (PATTERNS politics §7): a single snapshot runs ~5 minutes; an origin plus present
~12–16; a multi-segment investigation ~20+. Don't pad to a length; cut to the story.

## 7. Packaging checklist

- [ ] Five titles written; one chosen; promise matched by the film.
- [ ] Thumbnail brief (subject, text ≤ 4 words, signal element, palette); owner asked before creating it.
- [ ] Description with sources and timestamps; chapters; a companion drop if data was built.
- [ ] First 5 s show the thumbnail's promise; question by ~0:30.
- [ ] End screen planned; pinned comment written.
- [ ] Shorts / cut-downs identified from the blueprint (self-contained 30–60 s segments).

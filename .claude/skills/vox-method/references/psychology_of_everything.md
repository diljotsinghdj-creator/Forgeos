# Dragon Psychology: the psychology of everything

Dragon Psychology explains the psychology behind everything: everyday choices, money, love, work, crowds, scams,
habits, fear, status, memory. The owner described it on 2026-11-12 as "psychology of everything", broader than the
earlier brief of scams, biases and manipulation. Read this for every Dragon Psychology film, alongside the house rules
(faceless, no mascot, English, describe behaviour and never diagnose the viewer, dark psychology framed as recognising
and defending).

## 1. Why psychology needs its own evidence standard [O]

Psychology has more famous findings that failed to replicate than almost any other field. A film that repeats one
loses the channel's credibility for good. So every psychological claim carries a **replication status** in the dossier
as well as its source:

| Status | Meaning | How the film may use it |
|---|---|---|
| **robust** | Replicated across labs, or supported by a meta-analysis with a credible effect | State it plainly; show the size of the effect |
| **mixed** | Some replications succeed, others don't; or the effect is smaller than first reported | Say so on screen ("the evidence is mixed"); badge DISPUTED |
| **failed / contested** | Large replications failed, or the original study has serious method problems | Only as a story *about* the claim (why it spread, what went wrong); never as fact |
| **single study** | One study, not yet replicated | Name it as one study, with its size; badge REPORTED |

**Before using any famous finding, check its current replication status** in a recent meta-analysis or a large
multi-lab replication. Treat these classics as **check first**: ego depletion, power posing, the Stanford prison
experiment, the marshmallow test's long-term claims, the "10,000-hour rule", facial-feedback effects, social priming
(e.g. walking slower after reading about old age), "we only use 10% of our brain", learning styles. Most are
contested, failed or misquoted. That doesn't mean they can never appear; it means their status must be checked and
shown.

**Effect sizes, not just "significant".** Say how big an effect is in viewer terms ("about 4 visits a month, against
the 9 people expected"). Avoid "studies show" with no size or sample.

**Sources, best first:** meta-analyses and systematic reviews → large preregistered replications (Many Labs, Registered
Replication Reports) → the original peer-reviewed paper → university press releases (check them against the paper) →
popular books (never the only source).

## 2. Story shapes that suit psychology [O, with [V] from the case studies]

- **Live test on the viewer** (Live Viewer Trap): the viewer makes the choice and the film reveals the bias. It is the
  most native engine for this channel, and the Vox case studies show the test-before-theory pattern
  (are-we-automating-racism-2021).
- **The everyday object hides a mechanism** (Trojan Horse): the menu, the subscription toggle, the queue, the price
  ending in .99.
- **History echo:** a modern tactic set beside its historical ancestor (the mail-order club behind the auto-renew).
- **Escalation, then reframe:** everyday case → mechanism → high-stakes case → what it says about us.
- **Myth autopsy:** a famous finding everyone knows, what the replication found, and why the myth survived. This
  suits the channel and builds trust.

## 3. Visual language for the mind [O]

The mind has no native material to film, so this channel needs the most invention. Its visual identity is the look,
not a character.
- **Illustrated scenes and cut-outs** for behaviour (badged ILLUSTRATIVE when depicting real events).
- **Interface recreations** for choices made on screens (badged RECREATION).
- **Re-staged experiments:** the setup drawn or built simply, with the real result shown from the paper's own figure.
- **The paper as evidence:** the study's title page, figure and sample size, annotated (evidence first, as on Print
  Facts, where possible).
- **Effect sizes made physical:** unit charts of people, coins, days.
- **Visual metaphors,** one per idea, labelled as metaphors, never reused from the last film.
- **Avoid:** glowing-brain stock, brain scans used as decoration ("this part lights up" is rarely the explanation),
  faces of real private people, anything that diagnoses the viewer.

## 4. Ethics specific to the channel [O]

- Describe tactics and behaviours; never label viewers ("you are a narcissist").
- Dark psychology is always framed as recognising and defending, never as a how-to.
- Mental-health topics: no medical advice; point to professional help where relevant.
- Never normalise a step a real scam depends on (BRAIN_LOG 2026-11-10).

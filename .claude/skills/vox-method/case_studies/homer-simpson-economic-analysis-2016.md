## 1. Subject and central question
- [V] Uses the TV character Homer Simpson's long list of jobs (sources cite well over 100; one says about 191) to ask where his income sits relative to the real American middle class across the show's run. (Laughing Squid; AV Club)
- [V] The argument: Homer is a stand-in for the "middle class squeeze"; with inflation-adjusted pay, his income sits in the lower-middle bracket and stays flat. (AV Club; Laughing Squid)

## 12. What we can adapt (original equivalents, never copies)
- [O] **Proxy-person data set:** pick a well-documented figure (a historical ledger-keeper, a typical worker in an archival census record, a printer's apprentice) and run real statistics through their life to make a macro trend personal. Never a copyrighted character.
- [O] **Narrow the list on screen:** show the full messy list, then visibly cut it to a fixed, stated number with a rule ("only jobs held for a full episode"), so the method is part of the story.
- [O] **Price every row:** attach a real, sourced value to each item and sort it live into a single ranked bar, with our yellow #FFDD33 marking the reference band (median, poverty line).
- [O] **Receipts page:** publish the full table (dossier) alongside the film and say so in the closing line; the film carries the argument, the table carries the audit.
- [O] **Adjust before you compare:** always show the inflation-adjusted figure as a second beat (nominal fades, real slides in) so viewers see why the adjustment matters.

## 11. What makes it effective
- [I] It treats a hyped tool as a story with a lineage (captioning, early artists, then the 2021-22 wave), which gives viewers a timeline to hang the technical part on. It shows the product (images) as its own evidence. Inferred.
- [V] The producer later discussed videomaking, AI and science communication at length on The Gradient podcast (about 84 min, March 2024), a primary source for craft questions if accessed later.

## 12. What we can adapt (original equivalents, never copies)
- [O] **Reverse the solved problem:** open on a task the audience already accepts as solved (reading text from an image, matching a fingerprint) and ask what happens if you run it the other way.
- [O] **Lineage before novelty:** before explaining a new tool, show a dated precedent (an earlier experiment, an artist, a patent) so the "new" thing has a family tree.
- [O] **The tool is the evidence:** when the subject produces outputs, generate them on screen with the input visible, log every prompt/setting in the dossier, and label clearly. (Owner approval needed before any image generation, per our rules.)
- [O] **Concept-map metaphor:** visualise a model as a 2D field where nearby points are similar ideas, drawn in our own style, with a single #FFDD33 path showing one input being resolved.
- [O] **Spin-off for the stakeholder view:** a short companion piece for the affected group (artists, printers) rather than cramming it into the main film.

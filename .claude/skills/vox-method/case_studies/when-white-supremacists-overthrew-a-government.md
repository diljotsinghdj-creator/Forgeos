# When white supremacists overthrew a government

Evidence labels used below:
- **[V] Verified**: stated in a cited source (transcript, interview, companion article, credits).

## 1. Subject and central question
- [V] The November 1898 coup in Wilmington, North Carolina, when a white mob drove out elected Black and white officials and killed dozens or more (series listing; background sources).
- [V] The series exists to revisit overlooked moments of the past to give context to the present (series description via search summary).
- [V] The description says the event was buried for decades while its perpetrators were cast as heroes (via search summary).

## 2. Opening technique
- Not verified.
- [I] The title itself is the hook: a flat, factual sentence that sounds impossible for the US. Inferred from title.

## 3. Narrative structure
- [V] Covers the destruction of the Black-owned Daily Record newspaper and what losing its press meant for the community's ability to organise (aggregator transcript excerpt, paraphrased).
- [V] Includes the present-day voice of Cynthia Brown, a church historian whose ancestors lived in Wilmington (aggregator; Vox correction note).
- [I] The "missing chapter" frame implies a three-part shape: what happened → how it was erased or retold → why it matters now.

## 11. What makes it effective
- [V] It became a reference: Open Culture embedded it and paired it with the book *Democracy Betrayed*; HNN republished it; a Brown University resource collection lists it (search summaries).
- [V] Background sources note the event was long mislabelled a "riot" and is now understood as a massacre and coup (PBS American Experience; Smithsonian). [I] The film's power comes from correcting the label.
- [I] A living voice connected to the place turns an archival story into a present-tense one.

## 12. What we can adapt (original equivalents, never copies)
- [O] **The relabel.** Build a film around a name change: what the event was called at the time, what evidence now shows, and who changed the name. Show both labels on screen with dates and sources.
- [O] **The object that was destroyed.** Pick one physical thing lost in the story (a press, a ledger, a building) and explain what it did for people; its loss becomes the turning point.
- [O] **Present-day keeper.** Find a person who keeps the memory today (local historian, archivist) and use their voice as the bridge to now; for faceless films, use their recorded words over place imagery, with consent.
- [O] **Public correction protocol.** If an error is found after release, pin a timestamped correction (time, wrong word, right word) and log it in SEEN/dossier.

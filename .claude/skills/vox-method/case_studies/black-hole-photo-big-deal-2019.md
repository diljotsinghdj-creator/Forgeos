## 1. Subject and central question
- [V] How the Event Horizon Telescope collaboration produced the first image of a black hole, and why that matters: the image shows a region bounded by the event horizon, and the result is tied to Einstein's general relativity (rosetta.to summary).
- [V] The black hole is the one in galaxy M87 (background summary). Fong said she had assumed the team would show Sagittarius A*, the Milky Way's black hole, and had to adjust when M87 was announced (Open Notebook Q&A).
- [I] The central question is what we are actually looking at. Inferred from the summary's stress that the picture is real data, not an illustration.

## 2. Opening technique
- Not verified. No source describes the first shot.

## 3. Narrative structure
- [V] Covers: what a black hole and its event horizon are; why imaging it is hard; very long baseline interferometry (combining distant radio telescopes); how the data is combined; why the result matters for relativity (rosetta.to summary of timestamped highlights).
- [V] The summary says the image was assembled from scraps of light collected by radio dishes on mountaintops and combined in a supercomputer (rosetta.to summary; paraphrased).
- [I] Shape: object, then obstacle (it is too small and too far away), then ingenious method, then payoff and significance. A classic "how did they do it" structure, inferred from the order of the highlights.

## 12. What we can adapt (original equivalents, never copies)
- [O] **Pre-built evergreen core, swappable reveal:** for any scheduled event (a launch, a verdict, a data release), build the explanation weeks ahead and leave one marked "reveal slot" at the start and one "so what" slot at the end, with two alternate cuts for the likely outcomes.
- [O] **Illustration vs. evidence contrast:** open by showing how a thing has always been depicted (artist impressions, reconstructions), then show the first real record. This fits our evidence-first gate for Print Facts.
- [O] **Obstacle before method:** state in one plain sentence why the task looked impossible (size, distance, time, secrecy) before showing the trick that solved it.
- [O] **Revisit your own back catalogue:** keep a dossier of past explainers that a future event could reactivate; updating one earns speed without cutting depth.

# Why Nevada has so many ghost towns

Evidence labels used below:
- **[V] Verified**: stated in a cited source (transcript, interview, companion article, credits).
- **[I] Interpretation**: an informed production inference from verified material, reasoned not observed.
- **[O] Ours**: an original technique we derive for our channels.
Timestamps appear only when a source (transcript, chapter list) supplies them.

## 1. Subject and central question
- [V] Nevada has more than 600 ghost towns; why? (transcript excerpt via aggregator).
- [V] Answer centres on mining: a historian on camera says about 90% are directly related to mining (Yahoo Creators quoting Tami Force).
- [V] Vox's caption ties the towns to gold-rush-era booms of the 1800s and 1900s (mirror of the channel caption).

## 2. Opening technique
- [V] Leads with a striking count (600+) (transcript excerpt).
- [I] A big number about a place is the hook; the "why" in the title is the promise.

## 3. Narrative structure
- [V] The producer road-tripped through northern Nevada looking for lesser-known sites (Short description).
- [V] A local ghost-town historian, Tami Force, guides the explanation (Yahoo Creators).
- [I] Likely road-trip structure: each stop illustrates one stage of a boom-and-bust cycle. Background source (Nevada Magazine) describes that cycle (ore found → depleted → move to new claim), but the film's exact beats are not verified.

## 11. What makes it effective
- [I] A simple, countable mystery (why so many?) with a single dominant cause, told through places viewers can actually visit.
- [V] Sponsored by a tourism body, with a stated firewall on editorial control. [I] The disclosure protects credibility on a topic where the sponsor benefits.
- [V] Sources disagree on the exact count (600+ vs 800+ sites); the background source gives another comparison (ghost towns outnumber zip-coded places roughly five to one). [I] Any count needs its definition stated.

## 12. What we can adapt (original equivalents, never copies)
- [O] **Count-the-mystery hook.** Open on a surprising count about a place; define exactly what is being counted on screen, with source.
- [O] **One cycle, many stops.** Explain a repeating process (boom-bust, flood-rebuild, open-close) by showing a different place at each stage, so geography illustrates the cycle.
- [O] **Local keeper as guide.** Use a local specialist's recorded explanation as the guide voice; credit them on screen.
- [O] **Sponsor firewall card.** If a film is ever sponsored, state on screen and in the description who paid and that they had no editorial say; log it in the dossier.
- [O] **Shoot once, cut twice.** Plan the vertical Short in the shot list from the start (one standalone fact, under 60 s).

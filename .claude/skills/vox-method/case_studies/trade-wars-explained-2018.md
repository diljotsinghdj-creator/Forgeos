## 1. Subject and central question
- [V] What is a trade war, why do countries start them, and who gets hurt? Topical peg: 2018 US tariff threats against China and Mexico. (videosift description)
- [V] Two reasons named for tariffs: protecting domestic industry, or punishing another country. (videosift description; rosetta.to summary)

## 2. Opening technique
- [V] The description leads with the device: complex geopolitical manoeuvring explained through Game of Thrones. (thetvdb/YouTube description)
- [I] The hook is the analogy, introduced early so the abstract term "tariff" gets a fictional economy to live in. Inferred from the description's emphasis.

## 3. Narrative structure
- [V] Escalation logic in a caption excerpt: one house faces tariffs exporting to another, retaliates with its own, and if this keeps escalating it becomes a trade war. (VoiceTube excerpt)
- [V] Conclusion reported: trade wars generally hurt most participants, while countries outside the fight can sometimes gain new trading partners. (rosetta.to summary)
- [I] Shape: definition through analogy -> two motives -> tit-for-tat escalation -> who loses, who quietly wins -> back to the real 2018 dispute. Order inferred.

## 11. What makes it effective
- [I] A borrowed fictional economy lets viewers follow cause and effect without knowing real trade data; the "bystander wins" twist adds a non-obvious payoff to a short piece. Inferred.

## 12. What we can adapt (original equivalents, never copies)
- [O] **Self-built toy economy:** rather than borrowing a franchise, build a two-workshop or two-village model drawn in our own style (for Print Facts, e.g. two rival print shops) and run the mechanism there before showing the real case.
- [O] **Ratchet diagram:** each retaliation adds a notch to a visible ratchet or a stacked price tag; the tag on an ordinary item grows in #FFDD33 with each round.
- [O] **The third party beat:** after showing both combatants lose, pan out to a third actor who gains; one map, one arrow, one sourced figure.
- [O] **Two motives as two doors:** present "protect" vs "punish" as two labelled paths, then tag the real case with which door it went through, citing the official statement.
- [O] **Short-form discipline:** for a 5-minute piece, one mechanism, one analogy, one real case, one twist. Nothing else.

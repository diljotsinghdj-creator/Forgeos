## 1. Subject and central question
- [V] The video argues that Discovery Channel's Shark Week drifted from educational programming into sensational, partly fabricated "documentaries", centring on the fake Megalodon special and a fake film about a giant shark attacking a submarine (rosetta.to summary; search summary of Vox context).
- [V] Central question in the title: does Megalodon still exist? The summary states that scientists agree it died out roughly 2 million years ago (rosetta.to summary). Other sources give about 2.6 million years (Lead Stories fact-check), so the figure depends on the source.
- [I] The real question is about media trust, and the animal is the way in: the title asks a science question, and the body answers a media-literacy one. Inferred from the summary's emphasis on the channel's shift away from education.

## 2. Opening technique
- [V] Opens on enthusiasm, not attack: sharks are "really really cool", with three quick facts (about 400 million years, over 400 species, electroreception), then the turn that this is why it is baffling Shark Week has so little biology (transcript).
- [I] Praise the subject first, then indict its treatment: the opening sets the standard the channel is judged against.

## 3. Narrative structure
- [V] Chapter list (rosetta.to): intro 0:00 → Megalodon 0:49 → fake shark programming 1:32.
- [V] Order in the transcript: shark facts → Shark Week began in 1988 as educational, became sensational → attack-focused shows make people fear sharks, though attacks are rare → the 2013 Megalodon special: actor as scientist, faked footage, an invented death, only a "dramatized" disclaimer → scientific consensus that Megalodon went extinct about 2 million years ago, yet an online poll found 73% thought it real → it was the highest-rated episode, so the 2014 season doubled down (Lake Ontario promo hoax, *Shark of Darkness*, a Megalodon sequel) → counter-evidence of appetite for real science: BBC's *Life* drew over 11 million viewers, more than twice Megalodon → closing jab at Discovery's "number one non-fiction" claim (transcript).

## 12. What we can adapt (original equivalents, never copies)
- [O] **"Question title, different answer":** title a film with the naive question the audience types into search ("Is X real?"), then spend the body on the more interesting system question (why people believed it). Works for any myth, rumour or viral claim.
- [O] **Harm metric first:** before debunking, show one number that proves the false claim had real reach or real effect (views, share believing, money lost). For our channels: a single highlighter-yellow stat card, sourced in the dossier.
- [O] **Pattern of two:** never debunk one incident alone; pair it with a second, independent incident from the same source to show a pattern, and attribute both.
- [O] **Sub-three-minute debunk format:** one claim, one harm number, one second incident, one consensus statement with its source on screen. A fast-response companion to a long film.
- [O] **Show the other side:** our rules require the accused party's response; build a 5-second "their response" card into the template (in the Vox case, Discovery's eventual admission is the natural slot).

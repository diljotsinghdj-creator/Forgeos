## 1. Subject and central question
- [V] Roger Fenton's 1855 Crimean War photograph "The Valley of the Shadow of Death" exists as two near-identical exposures, one with cannonballs on the road and one with a clear road. Which came first, and was the famous one staged? (Kottke; Public Domain Review).
- [V] Darkroom as a series examines the untold history behind iconic photographs (thetvdb series description).

## 2. Opening technique
- Not verified at shot level.
- [I] The title is itself a yes/no mystery and the two-frame comparison is the obvious visual premise; the video very likely presents both frames early. Inferred, not observed.

## 3. Narrative structure
- [V] The video is built around a conversation: Lowndes talks with Morris about the mystery and how Morris reached his conclusion (Kottke).
- [V] Morris's path, as reported by Open Culture and others: a passage in Susan Sontag's *Regarding the Pain of Others* about early war photos being staged set him off; he travelled to the site in Crimea; early shadow-timing work was reportedly a dead end; with optical engineer Dennis Purcell he noticed small roadside rocks had shifted between frames; because disturbed rocks roll downhill, the frame where they sit further downhill must be later, so the cannonball version came second.
- [V] Even so, the reason the balls were moved remains uncertain (DIY Photography; Open Culture; Public Domain Review).
- [I] Structure is detective story: provocation (Sontag's claim), a false lead (shadows), the decisive clue (pebbles), a verdict with residual doubt. Inferred from the investigative sequence the sources describe; whether the video includes the false lead is not verified.

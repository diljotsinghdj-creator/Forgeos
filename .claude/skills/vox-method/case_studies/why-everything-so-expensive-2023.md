## 3. Narrative structure
- [V] Three competing explanations: too much money chasing too few goods, supply shocks, and corporate markups. (kottke.org; rosetta.to summary)
- [V] Diapers as a running case study: input costs (wood pulp, plastics) rose but are said not to fully explain the price hikes. (rosetta.to summary)
- [V] Remedies discussed: central-bank rate rises, increasing supply, and addressing corporate power. (rosetta.to summary)
- [I] Shape: one product -> three suspects tested against that product -> verdict that inputs alone fall short -> policy toolbox matched to each suspect. Inferred.

## 11. What makes it effective
- [I] A single, universally understood product carries a contested macro debate; each theory is tested on the same object, so the viewer compares like with like. Ending on remedies mapped to causes gives a practical close. Inferred.

## 12. What we can adapt (original equivalents, never copies)
- [O] **One object, three suspects:** pick one ordinary object and test each competing explanation against its actual cost breakdown, sourced line by line in the dossier.
- [O] **Cost stack vs shelf price:** draw the object's input costs as a stacked bar next to its retail price over time; the gap between them is highlighted in #FFDD33 and becomes the question.
- [O] **Suspect cards:** each theory gets a card with its best evidence and its weakest point; cards are revisited at the end with a verdict mark, including "partly".
- [O] **Remedy matches cause:** close by pairing each cause with the policy tool that targets it, and the trade-off each tool carries.
- [O] **Both sides of an allegation:** where a theory accuses firms (markups), show the firms' stated explanation (earnings-call quote, filing) on screen beside the critics'.

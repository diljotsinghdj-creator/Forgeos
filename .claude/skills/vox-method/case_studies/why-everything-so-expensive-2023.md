# Why is everything getting so expensive?

Evidence labels used below:
- **[V] Verified**: stated in a cited source (transcript, interview, companion article, credits).
- **[I] Interpretation**: an informed production inference from verified material, reasoned not observed.
- **[O] Ours**: an original technique we derive for our channels.
Timestamps appear only when a source (transcript, chapter list) supplies them.

## 1. Subject and central question
- [V] What caused the worldwide price rise of 2021-23, and can anything be done about it? Three explanations are weighed: too much money, supply shocks and corporate markups (transcript; kottke.org quoting the description).
- [V] The film says the data to decide between them may take months or years; it ends on policy tools rather than a verdict (transcript).

## 2. Opening technique
- [V] Cold open with the narrator on her way to the grocery store, bracing for "sticker shock", then buying diapers, the item where she notices inflation most (transcript).
- [V] Then a widening montage of voices: inflation as a worldwide phenomenon, a viewer-style question, and a list of examples (diapers in the US, food in Ghana, home prices in India) before the question is posed (transcript).
- [V] The narrator says later that she shot that grocery-store footage herself (transcript, "footage that I shot in the grocery store").

## 3. Narrative structure
- [V] Order in the transcript: grocery cold open → explanation 1, too much money, illustrated with a hypothetical car dealership, plus Larry Summers's warning about relief spending (quote stamped 00:01:31) → how the CPI is measured → the 2% Fed target (00:02:51) and categories that rose or fell → diapers as the test case, priced from NielsenIQ unit prices (00:04:12: 16.1 cents in 2019, 21 cents now, vs 17.4 at 2% and 19 at CPI) → labour and materials (wood pulp, plastics) as partial explanations → markups, via an interviewee who studied earnings calls, with quoted executives → recap of the three theories → tools: interest rates (with the early-1980s unemployment precedent), releasing supply (oil reserve; a 1939 precedent), and market concentration (70-80% of US diapers from two firms, 00:08:47).
- [V] The diaper concentration finding is presented as a turn: she looks back at her own grocery footage and sees the many brands belong to two companies (transcript).
- [I] Shape: one product, three suspects, partial verdicts, then remedies matched to suspects.

## 4. Narration characteristics
- [V] First-person narrator-reporter with a personal stake (she buys diapers and says that is where she notices inflation most) who shows her working: she checks the Pampers website for materials and explains that BLS does not track diapers, so she uses NielsenIQ (transcript).
- [V] One expert interviewee (named in the transcript only as Rakeen) on earnings calls; Larry Summers via archival clip (transcript).
- [V] The script admits a limit on the record: reporters do not know firms' true costs, so the film estimates (transcript, paraphrased).
- [I] The final line, that workers need not be hurt to bring prices down, comes from the interviewee; the film's own voice stays more cautious.

## 5. Visual techniques
- [V] Self-shot handheld grocery footage is used twice, at the start and again for the concentration reveal (transcript).
- [V] The narration points to documents on screen: "these are some transcripts of those earnings calls" (transcript).
- [I] Charts are implied for the CPI, the diaper unit price and the early-1980s unemployment line ("look what happened to unemployment"). Not observed.

## 6. Graphics and animation
- [V] Data the graphics would carry: CPI over the past couple of years with the stimulus-check timing marked ("here's when those stimulus checks went out"); category changes; diaper unit prices (16.1 / 17.4 / 19 / 21 cents); manufacturing wages up about 17% from 2019 to 2023; the producer price index for wood pulp and plastics; unemployment reaching one in ten by 1982 (transcript).
- [I] The four diaper prices are a natural counterfactual bar set (actual vs 2% path vs CPI path). Inferred; not observed.

## 7. Footage selection

## 12. What we can adapt (original equivalents, never copies)
- [O] **One object, three suspects:** pick one ordinary object and test each competing explanation against its cost breakdown, sourced line by line in the dossier.
- [O] **Counterfactual price bars:** show the actual price beside what it would be on a normal path and on the general index; the gap in #FFDD33 becomes the question.
- [O] **Your own footage returns as evidence:** plan a shot early that will be re-read later with new knowledge (a shelf, a street, a document).
- [O] **Suspect cards:** each theory gets a card with its best evidence and weakest point, revisited at the end with a verdict mark, including "partly" or "not yet known".
- [O] **Both sides of an allegation:** where a theory accuses firms, show the firms' own words (earnings call, filing) beside the critics', with dates.

## 1. Subject and central question
- [V] Who the factions in Syria's war are, how they emerged, and how a 2011 uprising became a conflict drawing in regional powers, the US and Russia (geographyeducation 2015; Aleteia 2015).
- [V] The 2017 version frames it as a proxy war with four sides, each with foreign backers, and covers the US strikes on Assad's forces (TheTVDB/2017 posts via search summary).

## 2. Opening technique
- [V] Coverage says the film starts from the first protests against Assad's government in early 2011 (geographyeducation 2015).
- [I] Starting at the protests (a concrete, datable event) rather than a thesis line suggests a chronological "how did we get here" opening. Shots not verified.

## 3. Narrative structure
- [V] Chronological: protests → factions emerge → outside powers join (geographyeducation 2015; kottke 2015 describes it as a 5-minute history of the war and the rise of ISIS).
- [V] A reviewer singled out its "very helpful timeline" of internal and external forces joining (geographyeducation 2015, short phrase).
- [V] It was re-made in 2017 when events changed (2017 posts refer to an updated version).
- [I] The structure is cumulative: each new actor is added to the picture, so complexity grows on screen at the pace the viewer can follow.

## 12. What we can adapt (original equivalents, never copies)
- [O] **Actor ledger.** For any many-sided story, build a running on-screen ledger (name, backer, goal) that grows by one entry per beat; never show the full ledger before the viewer has met each entry.
- [O] **Entry-order chronology.** Introduce actors in the order they entered the story, so chronology and cast list are the same thing.
- [O] **Version stamp.** When a film's facts age, re-cut and mark the new upload "updated [month year]" in title card and description; keep the dossier's change log.

# Syria's war: Who is fighting and why

Evidence labels used below:
- **[V] Verified**: stated in a cited source (transcript, interview, companion article, credits).
- **[I] Interpretation**: an informed production inference from verified material, reasoned not observed.
- **[O] Ours**: an original technique we derive for our channels.
Timestamps appear only when a source (transcript, chapter list) supplies them.

## 1. Subject and central question
- [V] Who the factions in Syria's war are, how they emerged, and how a 2011 uprising became a conflict drawing in regional powers, the US and Russia (geographyeducation 2015; Aleteia 2015).
- [V] The 2017 version frames it as a proxy war with four sides, each with foreign backers, and covers the US strikes on Assad's forces (TheTVDB/2017 posts via search summary).

## 2. Opening technique
- [V] (2017 version) Opens on the present tangle: after six years the war has four sides, each with foreign backers who disagree with each other, and Syria's chemical attack has just provoked the first direct US strike on Assad (transcript).
- [V] Then it states the method: to understand today's battle lines, go back to the beginning and watch how it unfolded (transcript, paraphrased).
- [V] The history starts with March 2011, when Assad's forces fired on peaceful Arab Spring demonstrators (transcript).

## 3. Narrative structure
- [V] Order in the transcript (2017 version): present-day summary → March 2011 protests → July 2011 armed rebellion and the Free Syrian Army → Assad releasing jihadist prisoners (quote stamped 00:01:09) → Jabhat al-Nusra (January 2012) and Kurdish groups → proxy war: Iran and Hezbollah for Assad, Gulf states and Turkey for rebels → 2013 chemical attack and the US debate (00:02:55) → ISIS's rise → US campaign against ISIS with Kurdish partners, Turkey's objections → Russia's 2015 intervention, bombing anti-Assad rebels (00:05:13) → Aleppo retaken (end of 2016) → spring 2017 chemical attack (00:05:42) and US Tomahawk strike → "another crisscrossing complication" (00:06:10) → no end in sight (transcript).
- [V] A reviewer singled out its "very helpful timeline" of forces joining (geographyeducation 2015, earlier pass; about the 2015 version).
- [V] It was re-made in 2017 when events changed; the transcript's opening is built around the April 2017 strike (transcript).
- [I] The structure is cumulative: each new actor is added, so complexity grows at the pace the viewer can follow.

## 4. Narration characteristics
- [V] Unnamed narrator; dense, date-led sentences ("in July", "that summer", "by the end of 2012") (transcript).
- [V] Archival audio of a US statement on responding to chemical weapons is quoted (transcript; quote stamped 00:02:55).

## 12. What we can adapt (original equivalents, never copies)
- [O] **Actor ledger.** For any many-sided story, build a running on-screen ledger (name, backer, goal) that grows by one entry per beat; never show the full ledger before the viewer has met each entry.
- [O] **Entry-order chronology.** Introduce actors in the order they entered the story, so chronology and cast list are the same thing.

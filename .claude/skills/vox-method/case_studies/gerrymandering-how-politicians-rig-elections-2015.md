Evidence labels used below:

## 1. Subject and central question
- [V] Ezra Klein explains how gerrymandering works and how to fix it (description).
- [V] Premise in the description: the US has 435 House districts that must be redrawn as populations change, and in most states politicians draw the lines.
- [I] Central question: "who draws the lines, and what happens when the players draw the field?" A mechanism-plus-remedy explainer, not a history.

## 2. Opening technique
- [V] Opens with a joke list of things Canada does better (poutine, politeness, a sitcom reference) ending on "elections", then the core inversion: voters should choose politicians, but in America politicians often choose their voters (transcript).
- [V] The history (Elbridge Gerry, 1810-1812) starts at 0:17 per the chapter list (rosetta.to).
- [I] Correction to the earlier inference: the film opens on a comic comparison and the conflict of interest, not on the neutral mechanism.

## 3. Narrative structure
- [V] Reported beats: the mechanism (districts, redrawing); a case study in North Carolina; an alternative in the form of Canada's independent commissions (Sanders Institute summary).
- [I] Classic three-part policy explainer: how it works, a worked example of abuse, a working alternative from elsewhere. Problem, proof, fix.

## 11. What makes it effective
- [V] Ecosystem: the video description links to a Vox card stack, "Gerrymandering, explained", a modular written explainer (search excerpt). Video and text are built as one package.
- [V] Advocacy groups on the left reposted it (NDRC, Sanders Institute), showing that a mechanism explainer with a remedy travels as a campaign tool. Our own films need to show both sides of a remedy to avoid being read the same way.
- [I] The comparative remedy (another country does this differently) turns a complaint into a solvable problem, which gives the film an ending.
- [V] Vox later returned to the subject in the Netflix series "Whose Vote Counts, Explained" (2020), a 21 to 25 minute format (Wikipedia; Vox post on X). The topic was scaled up, not replaced.

## 12. What we can adapt (original equivalents, never copies)
- [O] **Referee-is-a-player frame.** For any system where the party who benefits sets the rules (a species that shapes its own habitat, an industry writing its own standard), open on the neutral rule, then reveal who holds the pen. Mark the pen-holder in yellow #FFDD33.
- [O] **Problem, proof, fix.** Three acts: the mechanism in the abstract, one documented case with the original documents on screen (our evidence-first gate), and one working alternative from another place or era, with its own trade-offs stated.
- [O] **Toy model before the real map.** Teach the trick on a tiny invented grid (say 25 squares) where the viewer can count, then cut to the real document. The toy is ours, the real map is sourced.

# Divided island: How Haiti and the DR became two worlds

Evidence labels used below:
- **[V] Verified**: stated in a cited source (transcript, interview, companion article, credits).

## 1. Subject and central question
- [V] Why two countries sharing one island differ so sharply on development measures; a chapter heading asks why they are so different (aggregator chapter list; the only timestamp found places this question at 7:00).
- [V] Covers colonial history (French vs Spanish colonisation), cross-border trade, and recent immigration policy affecting people of Haitian descent (search summaries).

## 2. Opening technique
- [V] Opens on the gap in infant mortality, poverty and life expectancy between the two countries (search summary of the video).
- [V] Transcript excerpts show the host driving the road that separates the two countries, then starting at a beach village in southern Haiti where merchants, mostly women, set off (aggregator transcript excerpt, paraphrased).
- [I] So the film pairs a statistical contrast with a physical, on-location journey along the line itself.

## 3. Narrative structure
- [V] Sequence per summaries: development gap → on-the-ground trade journey (night-time boat crossings by Haitian merchants, the shared border market) → the "why" question (7:00) → colonial history → recent immigration policies.
- [I] The central question arrives near the midpoint, after the viewer has been immersed in the place. Inferred from the 7:00 timestamp in a ~16-minute film.

## 4. Narration characteristics
- [V] First-person host who travels and interviews (a caption: Harris interviewed a Haitian woman at the shared market).
- [I] The host's own route becomes the narrative thread ("I drove", "we started"), which is a Borders signature.

## 11. What makes it effective
- [V] Strong reach (about 8.6 million views on a listing) and an educator called it an exciting debut (search summaries).
- [V] Route chosen with audience input: Vox gathered more than 6,000 social-media suggestions to plan the series route (journalism.co.uk via search summary).
- [V] Accuracy exposure: a Reddit commenter claimed the film understated the size of Haiti's indemnity to France by roughly a factor of ten. One user's unverified claim, but it shows where history figures get audited.
- [I] It anchors a large structural question (colonial history) in specific people and a specific line on the ground.

## 12. What we can adapt (original equivalents, never copies)
- [O] **Twin-stat cold open.** Open on two places side by side with two or three matched measures; let the gap ask the question before narration does.
- [O] **Walk the line.** Use a physical path (a road, a river, a rail line, a coastline) as the spine; return to it between chapters as a visual reset. For faceless work, the path can be satellite/drone imagery or a map track rather than a host.
- [O] **Question at the midpoint.** Immerse first, then ask "why" out loud around 40-50% in, and spend the second half on causes.
- [O] **Money in history needs units.** For any historical sum, give original amount, unit and a sourced modern equivalent, and log all three in the dossier.
- [O] **Audience-sourced route.** Ask the audience where to look next; log each suggestion as a lead, never as a fact.

# Divided island: How Haiti and the DR became two worlds

Evidence labels used below:
- **[V] Verified**: stated in a cited source (transcript, interview, companion article, credits).
- **[I] Interpretation**: an informed production inference from verified material, reasoned not observed.
- **[O] Ours**: an original technique we derive for our channels.
Timestamps appear only when a source (transcript, chapter list) supplies them.

## 1. Subject and central question
- [V] How one island produced two very different countries, a poor and politically volatile Haiti and a stable, richer Dominican Republic (transcript).
- [V] The answer offered: colonial treatment (French over-exploitation of land and enslaved people vs a less exploitative Spanish colony), the isolation and independence debt imposed on the first Black republic, and present-day Dominican policy that strips citizenship from people of Haitian descent (transcript).
- [V] The closing frames the story as one of policy more than of a border (transcript, paraphrased).

## 2. Opening technique
- [V] The host opens in the present tense, driving the road that separates the two countries, and states three matched gaps: infant mortality (2.5 times), income (almost ten times poorer) and life expectancy (transcript; quote stamped 00:00:15).
- [V] He then states his question in the first person and starts the journey at a beach village in southern Haiti, where women merchants board boats for the border market (transcript).
- [I] The film pairs a statistical contrast with a physical journey along the line itself.

## 3. Narrative structure
- [V] Order in the transcript: the twin statistics → night boat journey (seven hours) with Haitian merchants → the southern border market, where Dominicans set up first while guards hold Haitians back (quote stamped 00:02:42) → "it embodies the asymmetry" (quote stamped 00:03:47), then the why-question → colonial history → independence, isolation and the debt to France → present-day citizenship ruling and deportations (quote stamped 00:09:39) → bus checkpoints in the DR → the deforested Haitian side vs forested Dominican side seen from the border road → the northern market, same pattern → closing argument → sponsor read.
- [V] CORRECTION: the earlier draft placed the central question at 7:00 and called it a midpoint question, based on an aggregator chapter list. The transcript shows the question follows the 3:47 line, roughly a quarter of the way into a 15:52 film.
- [I] Shape: immersion first (about four minutes in one market story), then history, then the present, then a second market that repeats the first as proof the pattern holds.

## 4. Narration characteristics
- [V] First-person host who travels, rides along and reports what he sees ("I quickly realize", "my journey starts here") (transcript).
- [V] Stand-up style pieces to camera are implied by lines such as "I'm looking across the border right now into the market" (transcript). [I] Whether he is on camera at that moment is not confirmed.
- [V] Interviews include the driver, who is also a Dominican border official, and a voice calling the citizenship ruling illegal, immoral and racist (transcript; speaker not named in the transcript).
- [V] The film carries an explicit moral judgement in its closing lines about centuries of racist policy (transcript).

## 5. Visual techniques
- [V] On-location reporting: the boat journey, both border markets, a border camp, a bus ride through checkpoints, the border road (transcript; also the repost's caption that Harris interviewed a Haitian woman at the shared market).
- [V] The narration invites a map-like view of the deforestation contrast: lush jungle on one side, bare hillsides on the other, then "zoom out a little bit and it's very clear" (transcript, short phrase).
- [I] That line implies a cut from ground level to an aerial or satellite view of the border. Inferred from the words; not observed.

## 6. Graphics and animation
- [V] An animation credit for Sam Ellis appeared in the earlier pass's search summary; not reconfirmed this pass.
- [V] Figures in the script that would need graphics: 2.5x infant mortality, ~10x income, the 1929 and 2010 constitutions, the 2013 ruling, more than 200,000 made stateless, over 55,000 deported, UN estimate of 128,000 who fled (transcript). How they are shown is not described by any source.

## 7. Footage selection

- [V] A woman taken to a military checkpoint by the bus driver, speaking Dominican Spanish and claiming her children are Dominican (transcript).

## 11. What makes it effective
- [V] About 8.6 million views (rosetta.to listing as summarised in search; the opened page confirms length and date).
- [V] Route chosen with audience input; Harris says audience members surfaced stories he could not have found by searching (journalism.co.uk, paraphrased).
- [V] Accuracy exposure (earlier pass): a Reddit commenter claimed the indemnity to France was understated. The transcript speaks of a debt to be paid over thirty years and gives no franc figure, so that complaint cannot be tested against this film's words.
- [I] A big structural question is anchored in specific people at a specific line on the ground, and the pattern is shown twice.

## 12. What we can adapt (original equivalents, never copies)
- [O] **Twin-stat cold open.** Open on two places side by side with two or three matched measures; let the gap ask the question before narration does.
- [O] **Walk the line.** Use a physical path (a road, a river, a rail line, a coastline) as the spine; return to it between chapters. For faceless work, the path can be satellite or archival imagery or a map track rather than a host.
- [O] **Immerse, then ask.** Spend the first quarter on one concrete scene that shows the problem, then ask "why" out loud.
- [O] **Show the pattern twice.** After the history, revisit a second instance of the opening scene to prove it was not a one-off.
- [O] **Money in history needs units.** For any historical sum, give original amount, unit and a sourced modern equivalent, and log all three in the dossier.

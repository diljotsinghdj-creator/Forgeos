Research notes for a neutral documentary. Compiled 2026-11-17.
Status key: **confirmed** (primary source or multiple independent reliable sources), **reported** (one credible outlet, or secondary coverage of a primary document not read directly), **contested** (sources disagree or claim is disputed), **opinion** (commentary or argument).

Method note: WebFetch could not reach niemanlab.org or scroll.in from this session (host not on the allowed network list), so those articles are cited from search-result summaries only and are marked "reported". ## 1. Engagement science: what makes people share

### Takeaway
Peer-reviewed work consistently finds that hostility toward an out-group, moral-emotional language and negativity all predict more engagement. The strongest of these is out-group language, at roughly 67% higher odds of a share per out-group word. None of these studies is about India or about hygiene content specifically, so any link to the genre is an inference.

### Cited Findings
- **Rathje, Van Bavel & van der Linden, "Out-group animosity drives engagement on social media", PNAS 118(26), June 2021.** They analysed 2,730,215 Facebook and Twitter posts from news media accounts and US members of Congress. Posts about the political out-group were shared or retweeted about twice as often as posts about the in-group. Each out-group word raised the odds of a share by about 67%, several times the effect of negative words or moral-emotional words. Out-group posts drew more "angry" reactions; in-group posts drew more "love" reactions. Status: **confirmed** (peer-reviewed; the 67% is the pooled estimate). Per-dataset ranges reported by PsyPost: 35–57% on one dataset set, 65–180% on the other. — [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8256037/); [ADS abstract](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021PNAS..11824292R/abstract); [BPS Research Digest](https://www.bps.org.uk/research-digest/social-media-posts-are-hostile-towards-political-opponents-get-more-shares); [PsyPost](https://www.psypost.org/hostility-towards-outsiders-motivates-engagement-on-social-media/)
  - Caveat: the data is observational and covers US politics, not national or ethnic stereotypes. It shows association, not causation.
- **Brady, Wills, Jost, Tucker & Van Bavel, "Emotion shapes the diffusion of moralized content in social networks", PNAS 114(28):7313–7318, July 2017.** In tweets on gun control, same-sex marriage and climate change, each additional moral-emotional word raised retweets by about 20%. Diffusion was stronger within ideological networks than between them. Status: **reported**. The 20%-per-word figure was confirmed only via secondary quotation of the paper, not read in the PNAS text here. Brady's own Scientific American piece puts it as "20 percent more likely to be shared". — [ResearchGate PDF](https://researchgate.net/profile/Jay-Van-Bavel/publication/317947723_Emotion_shapes_the_diffusion_of_moralized_content_in_social_networks/links/5f92152f458515b7cf96abb2/Emotion-shapes-the-diffusion-of-moralized-content-in-social-networks.pdf); [NYU CSMaP summary](https://csmapnyu.org/research/academic-research/emotion-shapes-the-diffusion-of-moralized-content-in-social-networks); [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/why-moral-emotions-go-viral-online/)
  - A later meta-analytic estimate, reported by Tech Policy Press, put the effect at about 12% per moral-emotional word, so the effect size is **contested** or smaller in replication. — [Tech Policy Press](https://techpolicy.press/psychologists-observe-moral-contagion-on-facebook-twitter)
- **Robertson, Pröllochs, Schwarzenegger, Pärnamets, Van Bavel & Feuerriegel, "Negativity drives online news consumption", Nature Human Behaviour 7:812–822, March 2023.** They used over 105,000 headline variants from the Upworthy Research Archive in randomised A/B tests, which control for story content. Negative words raised click-through rates and positive words lowered them. Sadness, not anger, drove the effect. Status: **confirmed** (peer-reviewed). — [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10202797/); [LMU open-access PDF](https://epub.ub.uni-muenchen.de/94951/1/s41562-023-01538-4.pdf); [Nieman Lab, Mar 2023](https://www.niemanlab.org/2023/03/negative-words-in-news-headlines-generate-more-clicks-but-sad-words-are-more-effective-than-angry-or-scary-ones/); [Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2023-03-headlines-negative-boost-consumption-online.html)
  - The study measures clicks on headlines, not shares of video, and does not examine disgust specifically.

### Inferences
- Clips that frame "Indians" as an out-group and invite disgust or moral condemnation combine the features these studies link to higher engagement: out-group reference, moral-emotional framing and negativity. This is an inference. No study found here tests India hygiene content directly.
- Hostile reactions still count as engagement. Rathje et al. found that out-group posts attract "angry" reactions, and those reactions feed ranking systems (see section 2).

### Gaps
- The per-word click effect in Robertson et al. is widely quoted as about +2.3% per negative word and about −1% per positive word. I could not verify these numbers against the paper text in this session, so check them in the PMC version before use.
- I found no peer-reviewed study isolating **disgust** as a driver of video shares on TikTok, YouTube or Instagram. Older work on disgust and urban-legend spread (Heath, Bell & Sternberg, 2001) was not retrieved here.

## 2. Platform evidence: ranking and payment incentives

### Takeaway
Internal Facebook documents show that the company weighted emoji reactions, including "angry", at five times a "like" from 2017. Its own researchers later linked angry reactions to toxic and low-quality content. The Haugen documents show that an India test account was flooded with hate within weeks, and that hate content in Hindi and Bengali went unflagged for lack of classifiers. YouTube and TikTok rank on watch time, interactions and satisfaction signals. Since October 2024, X has paid creators on engagement from Premium users.

### Cited Findings
- **Facebook's "angry" emoji was weighted 5x a like.** The Washington Post published "Five points for anger, one for a 'like'" on 26 October 2021, based on the Facebook Papers. From 2017, the ranking algorithm weighted all emoji reactions, including angry, at five times a like. In 2019, Facebook data scientists confirmed that posts drawing angry reactions were disproportionately likely to include misinformation, toxicity and low-quality news. Status: **reported** (WaPo based on internal documents; widely re-reported). — [MediaWell/SSRC index of WaPo article](https://mediawell.ssrc.org/news-items/five-points-for-anger-one-for-a-like-how-facebooks-formula-fostered-rage-and-misinformation-the-washington-post/); [The Week](https://theweek.com/facebook/1006422/facebook-reportedly-gave-the-angry-emoji-5-times-as-much-weight-as-a-like); [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/emoji-reactions-cute-addition-facebook-became-headache-rcna3747)
  - Later changes: in 2018 the angry weight was cut to 4x while other emojis stayed at 5x, and by the end of 2020 it was cut to zero. The Hill reports the other emojis were reduced to 1.5x in 2020. Status: **reported**. Sources vary on the exact intermediate weights. — [The Hill](https://digital-release.thehill.com/?p=578548)
  - Counter-view: Techdirt argued the Post's framing was misleading, because Facebook had said publicly that reactions would count "a little more" than likes, although it never disclosed 5x. Cato's Walter Olson noted that "angry" was the least-used reaction. Status: **opinion**. — [Techdirt](https://www.techdirt.com/tag/reactions/); [Cato](https://www.cato.org/blog/washington-post-facebook-promotes-outrage-isnt-outrageous)
- **Haugen documents on India: the Kerala test account.** On 4 February 2019 a Facebook researcher created a test account posing as a user in Kerala and followed every algorithmic recommendation for three weeks. The feed filled with hate speech, misinformation and violent content. The period overlapped the 14 February 2019 Pulwama attack. The documents say much of the content was "never flagged or actioned" because Facebook lacked "classifiers" and "moderators" in Hindi and Bengali. Status: **reported** (AP, Al Jazeera, NYT and others on documents disclosed to the SEC and Congress by Frances Haugen's lawyers, October 2021). — [Al Jazeera, 25 Oct 2021](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2021/10/25/facebook-india-hate-speech-misinformation-muslims-social-media); [AP via NBC Washington](https://www.nbcwashington.com/news/tech/facebook-dithered-in-curbing-divisive-user-content-in-india-2/2848890/); [The Federal explainer](https://thefederal.com/explainers-2/beheadings-anti-pak-posts-what-test-user-of-facebook-in-india-unearthed)
  - Facebook's response: it said it added Hindi hate-speech classifiers in 2018 and Bengali in 2020, and that the test led to "more rigorous analysis" of its recommendation systems. Status: **contested** (company claim, not independently verified). The internal memo itself said three weeks was not representative. — same sources
  - Relevance caveat: this content was mainly communal and anti-Pakistan hate inside India, not foreign "India is dirty" content. Use it to show that ranking systems can amplify hostile content and that moderation in Indian languages lagged, not as evidence about hygiene videos.
- **YouTube ranking.** YouTube describes its recommendations as optimising for "valued watch time", which combines watch time with satisfaction. Satisfaction is measured partly by post-watch surveys, and "Not interested" clicks are a negative signal. YouTube publishes no signal weights. Status: **reported** (secondary summaries of YouTube's statements). — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-provides-new-overview-of-how-its-video-recommendation-systems-work/); [Search Engine Journal](https://beta.searchenginejournal.com/how-youtubes-recommendation-system-works-in-2025/538379/)
- **TikTok ranking.** TikTok's help centre says user interactions, including likes, shares, comments, watch time and whether a video is watched to the end, are generally weighted more heavily than other factors. Status: **reported** (secondary summary of TikTok's help page). — [Hootsuite](https://blog.hootsuite.com/youtube-algorithm/); [CreatorDB](https://www.creatordb.app/blog/social-media-algorithms)
- **X creator revenue sharing.** The programme was launched in 2023. In October 2024 X changed payouts to depend on engagement from Premium users rather than on ads shown in replies. TechCrunch noted X did not say it would police engagement bait, and that rage bait already prioritised comments on Threads. Status: **confirmed** (TechCrunch plus X's help page). — [TechCrunch, 9 Oct 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/10/09/x-changes-creator-payouts-to-depend-on-engagement-not-ads/); [X Help: Creator Revenue Sharing](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/creator-ads-revenue-sharing); [BOOM web story](https://www.boomlive.in/web-stories/news/x-revamps-creator-payment-model-to-favour-engagement-what-about-content-quality-2180)
  - Eligibility per Scroll: a verified (Premium) account, more than 500 followers and 5 million organic impressions in the previous three months. Status: **reported**. — [Scroll](https://scroll.in/article/1072706/there-is-a-reason-your-x-feed-has-turned-more-toxic-have-you-heard-of-engagement-farming)
  - Later crackdowns, all **reported** in secondary coverage:
    - X said it would remove accounts that repeatedly solicit engagement from revenue share.
    - It said it would demonetise accounts that "programmatically" re-upload other people's clips.
    - In March 2026 it began prioritising home-region views, cutting payouts for accounts that farm views from abroad.
    - Sources: [Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/social-media/x-wants-to-stop-creators-from-farming-stolen-viral-clips-for-easy-money/); [Republic World, 25 Mar 2026](https://www.republicworld.com/tech/x-tweaks-its-revenue-model-shifts-creator-pay-to-local-focus-amid-us-iran-war-surge); [PiunikaWeb, Apr 2026](https://piunikaweb.com/2026/04/18/x-bans-tech-accounts-revenue-sharing/)

### Inferences
- Across platforms the shared currency is interaction: comments, shares, reactions and watch time. Ranking does not distinguish approving from outraged engagement, so a clip that provokes outraged comments from Indians and mocking comments from non-Indians is rewarded twice. This is an inference from ranking descriptions, not a disclosed platform finding.
- X's 2024 payout model ties money directly to engagement. That gives repost accounts a direct financial reason to caption clips provocatively (see section 5).

### Gaps
- No public document shows YouTube or TikTok specifically weighting "angry" or negative interactions, so do not claim an equivalent to Facebook's 5x.
- I did not retrieve the original WSJ "Facebook Files" pages (paywalled), or the NYT 23 Oct 2021 article "In India, Facebook Grapples With an Amplified Version of Its Problems". The NYT piece is a likely primary for the Kerala test and should be checked.
- No figures were found on how much X has paid India-based accounts. Linda Yaccarino reportedly cited a total paid to creators, but not by country ([The News Minute](https://thenewsminute.com/amp/story/news/x-has-paid-over-rs-166-crore-to-creators-so-far-ceo-yaccarino)); that figure was not verified here.

## 3. The creator economy: who makes these videos and why

### Takeaway
India is one of the largest creator economies in the world. YouTube says it paid more than ₹21,000 crore to Indian creators, artists and media companies in three years. Foreign travel vloggers' "dirty street food" and slum videos are a recognised and much-criticised genre with views in the millions. The "poverty porn" critique goes back at least to the 2009 debate over Slumdog Millionaire.

### Cited Findings
- **YouTube payouts in India.** At WAVES in Mumbai on 1 May 2025, CEO Neal Mohan said YouTube paid over ₹21,000 crore (about US$2.5 bn) to Indian creators, artists and media companies in the previous three years. He announced ₹850 crore more over two years. He said more than 100 million Indian channels uploaded content in the past year, and that about 15,000 have over 1 million subscribers. Indian content drew about 45 billion hours of watch time from viewers outside India in a year. Status: **confirmed** as YouTube's own claim; the figures are not independently audited. — [YouTube Official Blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/neal-mohan-creator-economy-waves-2025/); [Google India blog](https://blog.google/intl/en-in/products/platforms/youtubes-india-bet-inr-21000-crore-paid-out-to-indian-creators-commits-inr-850-crores-to-power-indias-creator-nation/); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/entertainment/youtube-paid-21-000-crore-to-indian-creators-over-the-last-three-years-125050100859_1.html); [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/05/02/neal-mohan-youtube-india-100-million-investment-creator-economy/)
- **Slumdog Millionaire (2008; Oscars 2009) and "poverty porn".** After its Indian release, critics and some public figures accused the film of feeding Western stereotypes and peddling "poverty porn". Amitabh Bachchan's remarks are often cited. Slum residents objected to the word "slumdog". NPR ran "Poverty Porn: Education or Exploitation?" on 5 March 2009. Status: **confirmed** that the debate took place; the critique itself is **opinion**. — [NPR, 5 Mar 2009](https://www.npr.org/2009/03/05/101487893/poverty-porn-education-or-exploitation); [Asia Society](https://asiasociety.org/node/13844); [Feet in Two Worlds](https://www.fi2w.org/best-picture-slumdog-millionaire-spurs-heated-debate-among-indians-about-their-countrys-image-news-analysis-from-fi2w/); [The Criterion (PDF)](https://the-criterion.com/V15/n6/FL03.pdf)
- **Slum tourism.** Dharavi tours grew after the film, though slum tourism dates to 19th-century London. Critics call the tours voyeuristic. Operators such as Reality Tours say they show Dharavi as a place of work and enterprise. Status: **reported** for tour growth (sourcing is weak); **opinion** for the ethical positions. — [Wikipedia: Slum tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slum_tourism); [YourStory on Reality Tours](https://yourstory.com/2016/05/reality-tours); [eTurboNews](https://www.eturbonews.com/15337/slumdog-millionaire-boosts-mumbai-tourism/)
  - A modern parallel: in 2023 the American TikToker Tara Katims was heavily criticised for her "slum tourism" posts from Dharavi. Status: **reported**. — [Fodor's](https://www.fodors.com/world/asia/india/mumbai/experiences/news/american-influencer-tara-katims-slammed-for-touring-dharavi-a-mumbai-slum)
- **Foreign vloggers and "dirty street food".** Several examples are documented in Indian media:
  - A creator, "Navety", went viral with a Kolkata video billed as India's "famous dirty street food" ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/lifestyle/viral-video-foreign-traveller-tries-famous-dirty-street-food-in-india-netizens-ask-how-sick-did-you-get)).
  - German vlogger Christian Betzmann faced backlash in January 2023 for ranting at street hawkers ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/latest/trends/story/german-influencer-abuses-indian-street-hawkers-gets-schooled-358694-2023-01-03)).
  - An Australian's "rich to poor in 2 seconds" India video went viral in July 2025 ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/latest/economy/story/this-country-can-go-rich-to-poor-in-2-seconds-aussies-brutal-india-video-goes-viral-484155-2025-07-11)).
  - Chinese vloggers were reported to have passed off Dhaka slum footage as New Delhi ([The Hans India](https://www.thehansindia.com/news/international/chinese-vloggers-misrepresent-dhakas-impoverished-neighbourhoods-as-new-delhi-make-disparaging-remarks-about-india-1140783)).
  - Status: **reported** (Indian lifestyle and entertainment outlets; view counts unverified).
  - Counter-genre: some foreign vloggers now market "the India they won't show you" (clean or modern India) as their own hook ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/lifestyle/the-india-they-wont-show-you-foreign-vlogger-breaks-stereotypes-shows-developed-clean-side-of-the-country)). Indian viewers called one New York vlogger's positive Delhi street-food video "rage bait for people who hate India" ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/lifestyle/rage-bait-for-people-who-hate-india-new-york-vlogger-tries-delhis-famous-street-food-and-loves-it)). Status: **reported**.
  - Unverified allegation: social media users claimed one vlogger, "Callum Abroad", was funded by a Pakistan-linked company. No evidence was found. Status: **contested/unverified**; do not repeat it as fact. — [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/viral/paks-isi-funding-western-tourists-to-malign-indias-image-foreign-vloggers-exploiting-poverty-narrative-exposed-by-netizens)

Status: **reported** (counts unverified). — [Brut India](https://www.brut.media/in/articles/viral-video-sparks-debate-on-street-food-hygiene); [Asianet Newsable](https://newsable.asianetnews.com/amp/india/viral-video-street-vendor-puts-hand-inside-his-pants-then-serves-samosas-to-customer-internet-reacts-watch-articleshow-m4w9gtv)
  - ThePrint opinion: India gets "unfairly bashed" for street-food hygiene, and Bangkok and New York are no different. Status: **opinion**. — [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/opinion/pov/india-gets-unfairly-bashed-for-unhygienic-street-food-bangkok-new-york-no-different/2338647)

### Inferences
- The supply side has a clear incentive: India offers cheap filming, high contrast and a huge diaspora and domestic audience that engages in large numbers, often by objecting. That outrage is itself engagement. This is an inference supported by sections 1–2.
- Many such clips are made by Indian creators for Indian audiences. Foreign repost accounts then strip that context. So the genre is not only foreign-made.

### Gaps
- I found no systematic dataset of view counts for the "India challenge" or "cheapest/dirtiest street food" genre, and no peer-reviewed study of it. Any view counts must be pulled from the specific videos, for example via vidIQ or the YouTube API, and dated.
- No Rest of World, Newslaundry or Scroll long-read specifically on this genre was found in this session.
- No figures were found for TikTok (banned in India since June 2020) or Instagram Reels payouts to Indian creators.

## 4. Selection bias: amplified content versus reality

### Takeaway
No study was found that measures what share of India-related social media content shows filth or chaos compared with India's actual conditions. What exists is evidence of real problems (food-safety failure rates of about 20%; see section 6) and evidence that hostile India-related content is concentrated and amplified by a few accounts (section 5).

### Cited Findings
- The NCRI study of anti-Indian content on X in 2025 (reported March 2026) found that amplification was highly concentrated. Three accounts reportedly drew over 10% of all likes and 20% of all reposts among the posts reviewed. Status: **reported** (secondary coverage; NCRI primary not read). — [IANS](https://ianslive.in/anti-india-hate-surges-online-amid-immigration-debate-in-us-report--20260312192351); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/immigration/indians-in-us-called-invaders-scammers-hate-posts-on-x-drew-300-mn-views-126031200623_1.html); [Ground News](https://ground.news/article/300-million-views-of-hate-how-2025-became-a-minefield-for-indian-americans)
- Travellers on Reddit debate the selection effect directly, for example the r/solotravel thread "Why do travel vloggers in India always show the…" (Feb 2024). Status: **opinion** (forum discussion). — [r/solotravel mirror](https://lr.eu.psf.lt/r/solotravel/comments/1akakxc/why_do_travel_vloggers_in_india_always_show_the/kp6lkit)

### Inferences
- The Robertson et al. and Rathje et al. findings imply that the negative slice of any country's footage will be over-represented in what spreads. That is a general mechanism, not an India-specific measurement.

### Gaps
- **No peer-reviewed content analysis was found** of how India is represented on YouTube, TikTok or Reddit (for example, the share of India-tagged videos showing poverty or dirt). This is a major gap. The documentary should not state a percentage.
- No study was found of the Reddit "Indian street food" or r/IndiaStreetFood-style subreddits, or of meme genres on r/2westerneurope4u and similar.

## 5. Rage bait and engagement-farming accounts

### Takeaway
CSOH and NCRI have documented anti-Indian racist content on X drawing hundreds of millions of views in 2024–2025, concentrated in a small number of high-reach accounts, with spikes tied to news events. Separately, journalists describe engagement farming on X as a business driven by revenue sharing. No published trace of a repost network built specifically on "dirty India" clips was found.

### Cited Findings
- **CSOH (Center for the Study of Organized Hate), Dec 2024–Jan 2025.** It analysed 128 anti-Indian racist posts on X with 138.4 million views, following the appointment of Sriram Krishnan as White House AI adviser and a post by Vivek Ramaswamy. It recommended that X ban newer slurs such as "pajeet". Most posts were still live when checked. Status: **reported** (advocacy and research NGO report; not peer-reviewed). — [CSOH press release](https://www.csohate.org/press-releases/new-report-surge-in-anti-indian-racism-and-xenophobia-on-x-after-krishnan-appointment-ramaswamy-tweet/)
- **CSOH, 1 Jul–7 Sep 2025.** It found 680 high-engagement anti-Indian racist posts on X with 281.2 million views. August saw nearly a fivefold spike over July, coinciding with US–India tariff tensions. Slurs appeared in about 18% of posts. Status: **reported**. — [CSOH report](https://www.csohate.org/2025/09/16/anti-indian-racism-on-x-july-september-2025/); [CSOH press release](https://www.csohate.org/press-releases/new-report-anti-indian-racism-on-x-on-the-rise/); [Free Press Journal, "Hate Sells"](https://www.freepressjournal.in/viral/hate-sells-x-sees-surge-in-anti-indian-rhetoric-says-report)
- **NCRI, 2025 full year (reported Mar 2026).** Weekly volume of anti-Indian content on X roughly tripled. More than 24,000 posts reached over 300 million views. Spikes were linked to immigration policy, including the $100,000 H-1B fee. Status: **reported**. — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/immigration/indians-in-us-called-invaders-scammers-hate-posts-on-x-drew-300-mn-views-126031200623_1.html); [IANS](https://ianslive.in/anti-india-hate-surges-online-amid-immigration-debate-in-us-report--20260312192351)
- **Engagement farming as a business.** Scroll explains how X's revenue sharing rewards accounts that farm replies and quote-posts, producing a "more toxic" feed. Status: **reported** (article not read in full; network blocked). — [Scroll](https://scroll.in/article/1072706/there-is-a-reason-your-x-feed-has-turned-more-toxic-have-you-heard-of-engagement-farming)
  - The engagement-farming ecosystem cuts both ways. Vatnik Soup, a blog-style source, says some farming accounts posting on foreign politics have been traced to India, Cambodia and Russia. Status: **reported/low confidence**. — [Vatnik Soup](https://vatniksoup.com/en/soups/368/)

### Inferences
- The anti-Indian racism reports and the hygiene-clip genre likely overlap: "dirty India" clips serve as visual "evidence" in slur-laden posts. This is plausible but not quantified in the sources found. Check the CSOH reports' example posts for hygiene and street-food imagery before asserting it.
- Event-driven spikes (H-1B, tariffs, appointments) suggest the clips are deployed opportunistically in political moments rather than shared at random.

### Gaps
- I found no journalist or academic trace of a specific repost network built around India hygiene or street-food clips, with account names, caption patterns and payout estimates.
- CSOH's and NCRI's sampling methods differ and are not peer-reviewed, so their figures are not comparable with each other.

## 6. The other side: real problems, enforcement and staged clips

### Takeaway
Food safety is a real problem. Government data shows about one in five food samples tested in 2023-24 failed standards, and FSSAI enforcement is substantial. At the same time, fact-checkers have repeatedly found viral "dirty food" clips that were scripted, AI-generated or filmed outside India, often reshared with communal framing.

### Cited Findings
- **FSSAI testing, 2023-24.** 170,513 samples were analysed and 33,808 (19.82%) were non-conforming. These split into 6,782 unsafe, 22,603 sub-standard and 4,423 with labelling defects. Status: **confirmed** (Lok Sabha answer annexure; marked provisional in an earlier reply that gave 169,725 analysed and 33,368 non-conforming). — [Lok Sabha / Dept of Consumer Affairs PDF, 2024 LS B 2538](https://fcainfoweb.nic.in/PMS/writereaddata/2024_LS_B_2538.pdf); [Rajya Sabha debate PDF, Aug 2024](https://rsdebate.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/750092/1/PQ_265_30072024_U902_p397_p401.pdf); [Lok Sabha 2025 reply](https://fcainfoweb.nic.in/PMS/writereaddata/2025_LS_B_361.pdf)

### Inferences
- A balanced framing would hold three statements together. Real hygiene failures exist (about 20% of samples fail). Platforms reward the most disgusting footage regardless of whether it is representative. And a measurable portion of viral clips are scripted, AI-made or from other countries.
- Domestically, staged "food jihad"-style clips appear to serve communal narratives rather than anti-India ones. The same footage can be repurposed abroad as "India is dirty" content. The documentary should keep the domestic and foreign audiences distinct.

### Gaps
- No estimate exists of what share of viral Indian hygiene clips are staged or AI-made. Fact-checks are case studies, not a sample.
- FSSAI data covers all food samples, not street food specifically. I found no national street-food-specific failure rate. FSSAI's "Clean Street Food Hub" certification numbers were not retrieved.
- Real sanitation context (Swachh Bharat, open-defecation data, WHO/UNICEF JMP figures) was outside this search and should come from another researcher's notes.

# The Indian accent as a punchline: media history, the call-centre era, and accent-bias research

Research notes for a neutral documentary. Compiled 2026-11-17.
Status key: **confirmed** (primary or multiple consistent reliable sources) · **reported** (single or secondary outlet, not independently verified) · **contested** (sources disagree, or the research has failed to replicate) · **opinion** (a commentator's or participant's view).
Search-engine summaries were used to find sources. Few pages were opened in full, so treat every figure as needing a final check against the linked page before broadcast.

## 1. The media history: from Sellers to Apu and after

### Takeaway
A recognisable "Indian voice" in Western comedy runs from Peter Sellers' brownface roles (1960, 1968) to Hank Azaria's Apu (1989/90 to 2020). Azaria is widely reported to have modelled Apu partly on Sellers. Hari Kondabolu's 2017 documentary turned the long-running criticism into a mainstream debate. Azaria left the role in January 2020 and apologised in April 2021.

### Cited Findings
**Peter Sellers**
- "Goodness Gracious Me" (1960) was a single by Peter Sellers and Sophia Loren, recorded to promote the film *The Millionairess*. In the film Sellers plays an Indian doctor in brownface. The producers declined to use the song in the film, but it was released as a single and peaked at No. 4 on the UK Singles Chart in late 1960. **Status: confirmed** (chart archive). [Wikipedia, "Goodness Gracious Me (song)"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodness_Gracious_Me_(song)); [Official Charts Company archive, 19 Jan 1961 chart](https://www.officialcharts.com/charts/singles-chart/19610119/7501). *Flag:* the summary I read described the song's lyrics as a "Cockney/Italian" pairing, which conflicts with the well-known Indian-doctor framing. Check the lyrics and sleeve before describing the song on screen.
- The song's title was later reclaimed as the name of the 1990s BBC British-Asian sketch show *Goodness Gracious Me*. **Status: confirmed.** [Wikipedia, "Goodness Gracious Me (song)"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodness_Gracious_Me_(song))
- In *The Party* (1968, dir. Blake Edwards), Sellers played Hrundi V. Bakshi in dark makeup, with a "heavily stereotyped Indian accent" and namaste greetings. PBS American Masters calls this the film's "inescapable and enduring problem". **Status: confirmed** (the casting); **opinion** (the characterisation). [PBS American Masters, "This caricature was once celebrated by a generation starving for representation"](https://www.pbs.org/wnet/americanmasters/this-caricature-was-once-celebrated-by-a-generation-starving-for-representation/33933)
- PBS reports that Satyajit Ray was offended by *The Party* and cancelled a planned project with Sellers. **Status: reported** (one outlet; the timeline needs checking, because Ray's unmade film *The Alien* fell apart for several reasons). [PBS American Masters](https://www.pbs.org/wnet/americanmasters/this-caricature-was-once-celebrated-by-a-generation-starving-for-representation/33933)
- The same PBS piece frames Bakshi as a figure some South Asian viewers embraced for lack of any other representation. It also says Jim Carrey imitated Bakshi at the Academy Awards. **Status: reported / opinion.** [PBS American Masters](https://www.pbs.org/wnet/americanmasters/this-caricature-was-once-celebrated-by-a-generation-starving-for-representation/33933)
- Critical defences exist. Saul Austerlitz is quoted as saying that "despite the offensiveness of Sellers' brownface routine, The Party is one of his very best films". Some reviewers say Sellers meant no malice. **Status: opinion.** [Nathan Rabin's Happy Place, 19 Oct 2022](https://nathanrabin.com/happy-place/2022/10/19/blake-edwards-1968-slapstick-masterpiece-the-party-is-an-utter-delight-with-one-huge-problem-in-its-unfortunate-use-of-brownface)

**Apu (The Simpsons)**
- Hank Azaria, a white American actor, voiced Apu Nahasapeemapetilon from the show's first season. Sources give either 1989 or 1990 as the start. Azaria has said he based the accent and mannerisms partly on Sellers' Bakshi. **Status: confirmed** that he voiced Apu; **reported** for the Sellers influence (widely repeated, attributed to Azaria). [PBS American Masters](https://www.pbs.org/wnet/americanmasters/this-caricature-was-once-celebrated-by-a-generation-starving-for-representation/33933)
- *The Problem with Apu* (2017) is a documentary written by and starring comedian Hari Kondabolu. It argues that Apu, especially his voice, became a template for taunting South Asian Americans. **Status: confirmed** (the film exists; its argument is the filmmaker's **opinion**). [Time, Apr 2018](https://time.com/5232896/the-simpsons-respond-apu-controversy). *Flag:* the commonly cited air date is truTV, November 2017. I did not verify it in a source opened for these notes.
- On 8 April 2018 *The Simpsons* replied in the episode "No Good Read Goes Unpunished". Lisa, looking at the camera, says: "Something that started decades ago and was applauded and inoffensive is now politically incorrect. What can you do?" A framed photo of Apu, signed "Don't have a cow", is on her nightstand. **Status: confirmed.** [Time, Apr 2018](https://time.com/5232896/the-simpsons-respond-apu-controversy); [The Ringer, 9 Apr 2018](https://www.theringer.com/tv/2018/4/9/17215174/the-simpsons-hari-kondabolu-problem-with-apu-response)
- Kondabolu called the response "sad" and wrote: "The Simpsons response tonight is not a jab at me, but at what many of us consider progress." **Status: confirmed** (his own words, as quoted). [The Ringer](https://www.theringer.com/tv/2018/4/9/17215174/the-simpsons-hari-kondabolu-problem-with-apu-response)
- In January 2020 Azaria said he would no longer voice Apu. **Status: confirmed.** [Global News, Apr 2021](https://globalnews.ca/news/7754709/apu-hank-azaria-simpsons-voice-apology/amp); [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/arts-culture/film-tv/hank-azaria-on-stepping-away-from-apu-role-on-the-simpsons-it-s-practically-a-slur-at-this-point-1.1202829)
- In April 2021, on Dax Shepard's *Armchair Expert* podcast, Azaria apologised. He said he felt he needed "to go around to every single Indian person in this country and personally apologise", called "Apu" "practically a slur at this point", and described reading, talking to Indian people and attending seminars. **Status: confirmed** (his own words, widely quoted). [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/arts-culture/film-tv/hank-azaria-on-stepping-away-from-apu-role-on-the-simpsons-it-s-practically-a-slur-at-this-point-1.1202829); [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/7754709/apu-hank-azaria-simpsons-voice-apology/amp)
- Reaction to the apology was split. Some commentators dismissed it as too late, for example reader comments on Dawn Images. **Status: opinion.** [Dawn Images](https://images.dawn.com/news/1186998)

**Other examples**
- *Indiana Jones and the Temple of Doom* (1984) was shot in Sri Lanka and the UK. Reports say India refused permission to film there because of the script's portrayal of Indians, including the "chilled monkey brains" banquet and the Thuggee cult, and that the film was briefly or temporarily banned in India. Actor Roshan Seth said the banquet scene was meant to send up the heroes' assumptions. **Status: reported** (no primary Indian government document was found; accounts differ on how long any ban lasted). [/Film](https://www.slashfilm.com/1326187/indiana-jones-and-the-temple-of-doom-banned-in-india); [/Film, banquet scene](https://www.slashfilm.com/1393592/indiana-jones-and-the-temple-of-doom-banquet-scene-joke); [Little White Lies](https://lwlies.com/article/indiana-jones-and-the-temple-of-doom-racist-indian-characters)
- Raj Koothrappali (*The Big Bang Theory*, 2007–2019) is played by Kunal Nayyar, who is Indian-born. Some Indian viewers and critics objected to an exaggerated accent and to the character's portrayal as unable to talk to women. **Status: opinion / reported.** [Cheatsheet](https://www.cheatsheet.com/entertainment/the-big-bang-theory-was-the-series-too-stereotypical.html); [Massey University thesis (Orientalist reading)](https://mro-ns.massey.ac.nz/handle/10179/10443)
- Baljeet (*Phineas and Ferb*) is voiced by Maulik Pancholy, an American actor who does not normally speak with an Indian accent. A student newspaper criticised the voice as an adopted accent. **Status: opinion** (thin sourcing: one student newspaper). [Andrews University Student Movement, 29 Sep 2021](https://andrews.edu/life/student-movement/issues/2021-09-29/why-baljeet-sucks.html)
- **Flag on the brief:** Short Round in *Temple of Doom* (Ke Huy Quan) is a Chinese character, not an Indian one. He belongs to the broader story of Asian caricature in Hollywood, not to Indian-accent mockery. Do not present him as an Indian-accent example.

### Inferences
- The same "voice" moves from Sellers to Apu, and then to playground imitation, which Kondabolu describes. That suggests the mockery is often of a performed accent, and that performance was frequently done by non-Indian actors.
- In each case the defence is "it was inoffensive at the time / no malice". The criticism is about cumulative impact. A neutral film can present both.

### Gaps
- The exact air date and network of *The Problem with Apu* were not confirmed in a source opened here.
- There is no primary source for Satyajit Ray cancelling a project because of *The Party*.
- No primary Indian government record of the *Temple of Doom* filming refusal or ban was found.
- No systematic audience or content study counting Indian-accented characters in US TV was found.

## 2. The call-centre era, tech-support scams and how they fused into the joke

### Takeaway
India's outsourcing boom (late 1990s to 2000s) created a training industry that taught "neutral" accents, and made the Indian voice on the phone a familiar Western stereotype. A parallel criminal industry followed: tech-support and fake-IRS scam calls run from Indian call centres, documented by the FBI, the CBI and Microsoft. That industry attached "scammer" to the accent, a theme in meme culture. Most of the meme-culture link is inferred; it was not directly measured.

### Cited Findings
**Accent-neutralisation training**
- A 2003 report describes Indian call-centre training institutes. A CEO defined a "neutral accent" as one stripped of "regional biases", and trainers said accent-switching could be taught. **Status: confirmed** (contemporary reporting). [The Tribune (India), 14 Jul 2003](https://www.tribuneindia.com/2003/20030714/login/main9.htm)
- Academic and ethnographic accounts describe agents adopting Western names and accents, and training as an identity shift. **Status: reported.** Example: "Indian by Day, American by Night" (law journal). [vLex](https://law-journals-books.vlex.com/vid/indian-by-day-american-night-55541296)
- In 2025 Teleperformance, the world's largest call-centre operator, began rolling out Sanas's real-time AI "accent translation" to soften Indian agents' accents. Reports cite a reported US$13 million deal. Sanas had earlier been criticised for a product framed as making voices "white-sounding". **Status: reported** (multiple trade outlets, citing Bloomberg). [CX Today](https://cxtoday.com/contact-center/teleperformance-uses-ai-to-soften-indian-call-center-agents-accents-and-so-does-its-competition); [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/technology/teleperformance-uses-ai-to-neutralize-indian-accents-of-agents-3425687); [HCAMag](https://www.hcamag.com/us/specialization/diversity-inclusion/call-centre-operator-uses-new-tech-to-soften-indian-agents-accents-report/527136)

**Scam calls**
- FBI IC3 2024 report: tech-support fraud losses across all ages were about US$1.46 billion. Victims aged 60+ filed 147,127 complaints and lost about US$4.8–4.88 billion in total, of which about US$982 million was tech-support fraud. Sources disagree on whether tech support ranked second for people over 60; investment fraud was first at about US$1.83 billion. **Status: reported** (secondary summaries of the IC3 report; the primary PDF was not opened). [InvestmentNews](https://www.investmentnews.com/industry-news/elder-fraud-complaints-surge-past-48-billion-as-investment-scams-lead-losses/260273); [Fox Business](https://www.foxbusiness.com/lifestyle/online-scammers-stole-record-16-6b-last-year-fbi-says). Primary: ic3.gov 2024 Internet Crime Report. **Important caveat:** IC3 does not attribute all tech-support losses to India. Scams also run from other countries and from the US.
- October 2016, Mira Road, Thane (Maharashtra): police raided call centres where callers posed as IRS officers and threatened US taxpayers over "unpaid taxes", collecting payment through prepaid cards and hawala. Hundreds were detained; reported counts range from more than 500 to more than 800 detained, with about 70 arrested. The alleged kingpin, Sagar "Shaggy" Thakkar, fled to Dubai and was arrested in Mumbai in April 2017 after being deported. **Status: confirmed** (the raids and arrest); **contested** (the scale: estimates range from Rs 25–30 crore in police filings to Rs 500 crore to "US$300 million"). [Scroll.in](https://scroll.in/latest/818299/scroll_in); [Business Standard / PTI, Apr 2017](https://www.business-standard.com/article/pti-stories/fake-call-centre-scam-mastermind-arrested-in-mumbai-117040800226_1.html); [Malwarebytes, Oct 2016](https://www.malwarebytes.com/blog/news/2016/10/multiple-scam-centers-raided-in-india-for-irs-fraud); [The Quint](https://www.thequint.com/news/business/mira-road-call-center-scam-how-millions-of-dollars-were-extorted-united-states-income-tax-blackmail-fraud)
- In October 2023 India's CBI ran "Operation Chakra-II": 76 searches after joint referrals from Microsoft and Amazon, targeting tech-support impersonation of more than 2,000 mainly US customers. **Status: confirmed.** [The Record](https://therecord.media/india-scam-tech-support-call-centers-raids-amazon-microsoft); [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/india-targets-microsoft-amazon-tech-support-scammers-in-nationwide-crackdown/)
- In 2025 Operation Chakra-V targeted call centres defrauding Japanese victims (May) and UK and Australian victims (July, Noida, with the NCA, FBI and Microsoft). Microsoft says its referrals have contributed to more than 30 raids and more than 100 arrests. **Status: confirmed** (CBI and Microsoft statements via press). [The Hacker News, Jul 2025](https://thehackernews.com/2025/07/cbi-shuts-down-390k-uk-tech-support.html); [The Hacker News, Jun 2025](https://thehackernews.com/2025/06/microsoft-helps-cbi-dismantle-indian.html)
- The FBI, Indian police and CBI shut down three India-based call centres tied to about US$48.8 million in losses from about 660 US victims of government-impersonation and tech-support scams since 2022. **Status: reported** (date and press release not verified; the outlets are syndicated). [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/policy/articles/fbi-shuts-down-3-india-130100119.html); [Outlook India](https://www.outlookindia.com/international/us-shuts-down-india-based-call-centre-scam-that-targeted-elderly-americans)
- In Microsoft's 2021 Global Tech Support Scam Research (YouGov, 16 countries, 16,254 adults), 59% had encountered a tech-support scam in the past 12 months, down from 64% in 2018. 16% continued engaging with the scammer and 7% lost money. India had among the highest encounter rates, 69%: Indians are also major targets, not just a source. **Status: confirmed** (Microsoft's own survey). [Microsoft News, 22 Jul 2021](https://news.microsoft.com/source/asia/2021/07/22/tech-support-scams-remain-a-threat-globally-and-in-asia-pacific-despite-drop-in-encounters-microsoft-survey/); [Microsoft India](https://news.microsoft.com/en-in/seven-out-of-10-consumers-in-india-encountered-tech-support-scams-in-the-past-year-microsoft-survey/)

### Inferences
- Two distinct groups share one stereotype. The first is millions of legitimate Indian BPO workers, who were trained to sound less Indian. The second is a criminal subset that impersonates Western institutions. The accent joke folds both into one "tech support" figure. That is a balance point for the film.
- Indian victims (69% encounter rate) and Indian enforcement (CBI raids) complicate a simple "India = scammers" framing.

### Gaps
- **FTC data:** I did not retrieve FTC Consumer Sentinel figures on IRS/government-impostor calls or their origin. The primary FTC/TIGTA data on the IRS phone scam, roughly 2013–2017, is still to be pulled.
- The primary IC3 PDF was not opened, and IC3 does not break down losses by the scammer's country.
- No academic study was found that directly links scam-call exposure to accent mockery online, for example meme corpus analysis of "scammer" or "Do the needful". The fusion is an inference.
- I did not get BPO employment figures for the 2000s (e.g. NASSCOM).

## 3. What linguistics research says about accent bias

### Takeaway
Linguists treat accent bias as real and measurable. Lippi-Green's "standard language ideology" explains it as social, not linguistic. Experimental work finds penalties for accented speakers in credibility and hiring, including Indian-accented English specifically. One headline study, Lev-Ari & Keysar 2010, has replication problems. Kachru's World Englishes framework treats Indian English as a nativised variety with its own norms. About 129 million Indians reported speaking English in the 2011 Census.

### Cited Findings
- Rosina Lippi-Green, *English with an Accent: Language, Ideology, and Discrimination in the United States* (Routledge, 1997; 2nd ed. 2012), argues that a "standard language ideology" treats one variety as neutral and others as deficient. Her analysis of 38 Disney animated films and more than 350 characters found that characters with foreign accents were disproportionately cast as villains or untrustworthy. **Status: confirmed** (the book's content, per reviews; the exact counts should be checked against the book). [Cal State Fullerton review (PDF)](https://english.fullerton.edu/publications/clnArchives/pdf/wynnEng.pdf); [JSTOR Daily](https://daily.jstor.org/very-british-villains-and-other-anglo-saxon-attitudes-to-accents/)
- Lev-Ari, S. & Keysar, B. (2010), "Why don't we believe non-native speakers? The influence of accent on credibility", *Journal of Experimental Social Psychology* 46, 1093–1096. Listeners rated trivia statements as less true when read by non-native speakers, even though the speakers were only reading facts. When told about the difficulty, listeners corrected the effect for mild accents but not for heavy ones. The authors attribute this to processing fluency, not prejudice alone. **Status: confirmed** (it was published); **contested** (replication). [Paper PDF (MPG)](https://pure.mpg.de/pubman/item/item_1837436_6/component/file_1837698/LevAriKeysar.pdf); [ScienceDaily, 19 Jul 2010](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2010/07/100719164002.htm)
- A later three-study replication using a near-identical method failed to reproduce the credibility penalty. A 2021 abstract co-authored by Lev-Ari also says the finding "has not always been replicated", and reports that exposure to an accent reduces bias. **Status: contested.** [eScholarship replication (PDF)](https://escholarship.org/content/qt8hd9v4ff/qt8hd9v4ff.pdf); [CUNY 2021 abstract (Grabka & Lev-Ari)](https://www.cuny2021.io/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/CUNY_2021_abstract_20.pdf). **Flag:** if the film cites Lev-Ari & Keysar, it must say the effect is disputed.
- Hiring: Timming (2017), *Work, Employment and Society*. US managers in simulated telephone interviews rated applicants with Chinese-, Mexican- and Indian-accented English lower, especially for customer-facing roles. **Status: confirmed** (peer-reviewed abstract). [University of St Andrews research portal](https://research-portal.st-andrews.ac.uk/en/publications/the-effect-of-foreign-accent-on-employability-a-study-of-the-aura/); [OnLabor summary](https://onlabor.org/the-sound-of-discrimination-how-employment-law-handles-linguistic-profiling/)
- A UK study (Queen Mary University of London, the "Accent Bias in Britain" project) found accent bias in mock hiring by 61 lawyers and recruiters. The page I read did not give Indian-specific results. **Status: confirmed** (the project); Indian-specific findings are a gap. [QMUL](https://www.qmul.ac.uk/arts/research/featured-research/could-accent-bias-harm-your-job-prospects/)
- A 2024 study reported by Business Insider India found South Asian/Indian accents treated more negatively in hiring than other accents, with HR professionals showing bias similar to lay raters. **Status: reported** (authors not named in the article; unverified). [Business Insider India](https://www.businessinsider.in/careers/interviewing/having-an-regional-accent-can-be-bad-for-your-interviews-especially-an-indian-one-study/amp_articleshow/109644176.cms)
- A 2025 IZA discussion paper (Taveras, Tonguc, Zhu & Miller) experimentally tests accent-based hiring discrimination. I did not read the findings. **Status: reported** (exists). [IZA DP 18239](https://docs.iza.org/dp18239.pdf)
- Braj Kachru's "Three Circles" model (1985) places India in the Outer Circle: countries where English is institutionalised through colonial history and is "norm-developing", not merely "norm-dependent". His 1965 article "The Indianness in Indian English" laid the groundwork. Randolph Quirk argued that Outer Circle varieties were "interference varieties", and Kachru rebutted him (the Quirk–Kachru debate, c. 1990). **Status: confirmed** (the model and debate are well documented; the "legitimate variety" framing is the World Englishes school's **opinion**, contested by Quirk). [Wikipedia, Braj Kachru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braj_Kachru); [Purdue OWL, World Englishes](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/multilingual/world_englishes); [IH Journal](https://ihworld.com/ih-journal/issues/issue-50/standard-international-english-part-1/)
- Census of India 2011: about 129 million people (≈10.6%) reported speaking English. About 260,000 gave it as their mother tongue, about 83 million as a second language and about 46 million as a third. That makes English India's second most widely spoken language after Hindi. **Status: reported** (the figures come from secondary summaries of the census bilingualism tables C-17; check against censusindia.gov.in). Commentators note that self-reported census language data may under-count actual use. [Census of India 2011 tables (ORGI)](https://censusindia.gov.in/nada/index.php/catalog/43470); [Wikipedia, Multilingualism in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multilingualism_in_India); [The Diplomat, Jul 2018](https://thediplomat.com/2018/07/indias-evolving-linguistic-landscape)

### Inferences
- India holds one of the largest English-speaking populations in the world, depending on how proficiency is defined. That sits uneasily with the joke's premise that the accent signals poor English, and it is a natural "turn" in the script.
- The research supports "accent bias exists in hiring" more firmly than "accented speech is believed less". The second is disputed.

### Gaps
- I did not open the Lippi-Green text directly. Quote the page numbers from the book.
- No peer-reviewed study specifically on Indian-English credibility (as opposed to hiring) was found.
- India's 2021 census has not been conducted, so 2011 is the latest official figure.

## 4. Counterpoints: Indian-heritage comedians and the accent

### Takeaway
Comedians of Indian heritage, most prominently Russell Peters, have built careers partly on imitating their parents' accents. Peters defends this as truthful family observation. Critics argue it gave non-South Asian audiences licence to repeat "the voice". There is no systematic audience research settling whether it reclaims or reinforces.

### Cited Findings
- Russell Peters, a Canadian comedian of Anglo-Indian heritage, defends his accent material by comparing it to a white comic doing a Boston accent to imitate his father: "No, I'm just being true to what I'm talking about." **Status: opinion** (Peters' own words). [The Daily Beast](https://thedailybeast.com/russell-peters-defends-comedic-stereotypes-nothing-hacky-about-indian-accent)
- Peters on stereotypes: "There has to be something that happened that made people go 'that's a pretty good stereotype right there'… But then you have to deconstruct it." **Status: opinion.** [Quartz India interview](https://qz.com/india/1633635/almost-famous-star-russell-peters-on-politics-and-religion)
- A South Asian TikTok creator criticised Peters, Mindy Kaling and Lilly Singh for using "the voice". Another critic recalled white classmates quoting Peters lines at them in school. **Status: opinion.** [Yahoo News](https://news.yahoo.com/mindy-kaling-other-south-asian-215721954.html)
- NPR Code Switch devoted an episode to the question, "What's So Funny About The Indian Accent?" (31 Aug 2016). **Status: confirmed** (it exists). [NPR / NPR Illinois](https://www.nprillinois.org/equity-justice/2016-08-31/whats-so-funny-about-the-indian-accent-episode-15)
- Academic work on ethnic comedy (a 2012 paper on Peters) notes that audiences can read the subversion of a stereotype as confirmation of it, which makes such comedy "double-edged". The core evidence comes from studies of Black comedians and is applied to Peters only by extension. **Status: opinion / reported.** [Keio University paper (PDF), 2012](https://www.flang.keio.ac.jp/webfile/AWC/AWC2012/2012UF-MA.pdf)
- Kondabolu himself argues in *The Problem with Apu* that who performs the accent, and for whom, matters. **Status: opinion.** [The Ringer](https://www.theringer.com/tv/2018/4/9/17215174/the-simpsons-hari-kondabolu-problem-with-apu-response)

### Inferences
- The "in-group vs out-group" distinction (Peters vs Azaria) is the most defensible neutral framing. Both camps exist within South Asian diaspora commentary.

### Gaps
- No quantitative audience study on whether in-group accent comedy changes out-group attitudes towards Indians was found.
- I did not cover the British angle in depth: *Goodness Gracious Me* (BBC, 1998–2001), *The Kumars at No. 42*, and the 1970s sitcom *It Ain't Half Hot Mum*, in which Michael Bates, a white actor, played an Indian character. These are worth a follow-up search.
- No sourced data on online meme formats ("do the needful", "sir please redeem", scam-baiter YouTube channels) was found. A separate research task is needed if the film covers meme culture.

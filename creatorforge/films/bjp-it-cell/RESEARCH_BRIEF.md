# The BJP IT Cell: research brief

The evidence file is `dossier.json`: 38 claims and 78 sources, each claim with a status. This brief is the readable
version. **C##** refers to a claim in the dossier.

**Working question:** *How did a party's social media team become one of India's most powerful political machines,
and what does it actually do?*

## The status rules the film follows

| Status | How the film may say it |
|---|---|
| verified | As fact |

| allegation | "[Person/outlet] alleges…", followed by the response |
| opinion | "[Person] says…", naming who they are |

## 2. Strongest material, by act

**Hook.** In Amit Shah's own words, on the BJP's own video (C07): *"we can make any message viral, true or false."*
It's the party's own upload, so it's hard to dispute. Use the exact Hindi words and their full context.

**Act 1: Birth.**
- Founded in 2007 (C01).
- Its founder later turned critic: Bora's "Frankenstein's monster" (C06). Say on screen that he now leads a rival party.

**Act 2: The machine.** Scale, mostly in the party's own numbers:
- 32 lakh members in two WhatsApp groups (C07).
- About 9 lakh booth-level phone volunteers (C08).
- In Bihar, 9,500 IT-cell heads and 72,000 to 1 lakh+ groups (C09).
- In Karnataka, about 150,000 workers (C10).
- In Mandi, 400+ groups in one small town (C11).
- On a platform with about 500 million Indian users (C12).

**Act 3: The power.**
- Digital ad dominance in 2024 (C30), compared with 2019 (C38).
- Twitter-trend campaigns (C14–15).

**Act 4: The accusations.** Every one attributed, every one with its response.

| Accusation | Source | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Fake or manipulated media from the cell's head | Twitter's own label (C19); fact-check tallies (C21) | Ask Malviya |
| "Hit list" of journalists | Chaturvedi's book (C26) | BJP: the witness was never in the cell |
| PM following abusive accounts | Alt News and others (C27) | BJP: "mischievous"; following isn't endorsing |
| "The IT cell has gone rogue" | BJP's own MP, Swamy (C28) | Party silent; Swamy often breaks ranks |
| Platform favouritism | WSJ (C22), whistleblowers (C23–24), Al Jazeera (C25) | Meta: rules applied uniformly |
| Coordinated trends | Peer-reviewed study (C14) | Researchers say "most likely" the BJP; not proven (C15) |

**Act 5: The other side.**
- Every party does it:
  - Facebook removed far more Congress-linked accounts than BJP-linked ones in 2019 (C18).
  - Congress workers were arrested over a fake Amit Shah video (C32).
  - Congress's own ex-digital head said she was trolled by her own party (C31).
  - Oxford found "junk news" on both parties' pages (C16).
- The accusation that collapsed: Tek Fog (C34). Tell it honestly. It's what keeps the film credible.
- The BJP's case (C36): organised political communication by volunteers.

**Ending.**
- Malviya's exit in August 2026 (C04–05): the end of an era, or the same machine under a new name?
- The open question: who decides what's fake (C35)?

## 3. Red lines (legal and ethical)

1. **Tek Fog is never evidence** (C34). It was retracted.
2. **Amnesty's abuse figures (C29) are not about the IT cell.** Never imply they are.
3. **No ordinary account holders:** no names, handles or faces.
4. **The MP in the Sophie Zhang allegation (C23):** don't name him without a lawyer's advice.
5. **Shah's Kota speech (C07):** play enough context that the meaning isn't changed.
6. **State units (Assam, C33) and spokespeople (Patra, C20) are not the national IT cell:** say so.
7. **Right of reply:** before release, send written questions to the BJP IT Cell (Deepak Mhaskey) and Amit Malviya, and
   quote their answers or say they didn't reply.

## 5. What makes this film strong

The film doesn't need to say "the BJP is evil". The strongest facts are the party's own:
- its president's words;
- its own volunteer numbers;
- a platform label on its IT head's post;
- its own MP's criticism.

Then it shows the system is bigger than one party, and it tells the retracted story honestly. That makes it hard to
dismiss as propaganda, and it protects you in court.

# Disgust Pays (working title): script, draft 1

- **Channel:** Print Facts, long form, 16:9, about 9 minutes.
- **Style:** the Vox × Johnny Harris five-beat structure and visuals, with narration in a John Oliver satirical voice
  (MASTER_DIRECTIVES §10). Each point runs fact, then the receipt on screen, then a comparison, then the snap back.
- **The pick:** the owner's own draft (2026-11-17), corrected against `reports/Why India is mocked online.md`.
- **Numbers:** every number is in `dossier.json`. Lines marked ⚠ rest on press reports and must be checked before air.
- **Bracket notes:**
  - `[FRAME]`: the key frame on screen.
  - `[SFX]`: analog foley only.
  - `[CUT]`: the hard music cut, 0.2 s of silence before a punchline.
- **Callbacks:**
  - "the drain inspector" (Gandhi, 1927), set up in act 2 and paid off at the end;
  - "the same four clips", from the cold open, paid off in the faces grid;
  - "Extraordinary Achievements", the TRAI slide title, which comes back for the soft-power cards.

## 1 · HOOK AND QUESTION (0:00–1:10)

**[FRAME 01_feed]** `[SFX: thumb swipes, notification ticks, building]`
If the feed were your only window on India, you'd think a country of about 1.4 billion people was four clips on a loop: traffic,
a crowded train, and two men making street food with what can only be described as structural confidence.

`[SFX: swipes stop dead]` `[CUT]`
That is not a country. That is a highlight reel edited by someone who hates you.

**[FRAME 02_receipt]** `[SFX: paper slapped on a desk]`
I went looking for a number, and it turns out somebody had already counted. In December 2024, after a White House tech
appointment, a research group called the Center for the Study of Organized Hate logged 128 posts on X aimed at Indians.
Together they had 138.54 million views.

That's not a fringe. That's a mid-sized country's worth of eyeballs, pointed at a slur.

**[FRAME 03_otherfeed]**
Meanwhile, off the feed:
- India landed a spacecraft near the Moon's south pole on 23 August 2023.
- It ran 25.47 billion digital payments in a single month. ⚠
- It counts 3,682 wild tigers, about three-quarters of the world's.

`[CUT]` None of it trended. Apparently a tiger doesn't get the engagement of a man frying something questionable.

**[FRAME 04_title]** `[SFX: one heavy book dropped flat]`
So tonight: why the internet keeps showing you the worst of India, who it pays, and why it's an old story wearing a new phone.

## 2 · MAP AND HISTORY (1:10–3:20)

**[FRAMES 05_map → globe_a/b/c → ship_a/b/c]**
To understand the feed, you have to go back to a company. In 1600 a group of London merchants got a royal charter to trade with
the East. Their ships went the long way round, past the Cape of Good Hope, and by 1690 Company traders had settled at a place
called Calcutta.

`[CUT]` Its mission statement was "trade". Approximately.

By 1858 the British Crown had taken over directly. And when you run someone else's country, you need a story about why that's a
kindness.

**[FRAMES 06_macaulay → z06_smash]** `[SFX: page turn]`
Here's one of the receipts. In 1835 Thomas Babington Macaulay wrote that "a single shelf of a good European library was worth the
whole native literature of India and Arabia." A confident review. Then, in the same document:

`[SMASH-ZOOM]` `[CUT]`
"I have no knowledge of either Sanscrit or Arabic."

That is a man giving one star to a library he admits he has never read. If he wrote Yelp reviews, he'd have been banned.

**[FRAME 07_drain]**
In 1927 an American writer, Katherine Mayo, published *Mother India*, a bestseller about everything wrong with the country.
Gandhi reviewed it, and he called it "the report of a drain inspector sent out with the one purpose of opening and examining the
drains of the country."

`[CUT]` Remember that phrase. A drain inspector. He named the genre ninety-eight years before the algorithm monetised it.

**[FRAME 08_worlds]** `[SFX: chalk]`
Then the vocabulary got an update. In 1952 the French demographer Alfred Sauvy coined "Third World", and it meant countries
aligned with neither Cold War bloc. Somewhere along the way, Western usage quietly changed it to mean "poor".

`[CUT]` A definition, updated without notice. Like a terms-of-service email nobody read.

## 3 · MECHANICS (3:20–5:30)

**[FRAME 08b_data]**
Now the plot twist. Mobile data in India cost about ₹269 a gigabyte in 2014. By 2020 it was about ₹11, according to the regulator.
A new 4G network, Jio, launched in 2016 with free data, and internet subscribers went from 239 million to 776 million.

Hundreds of millions of people came online and started filming their lives. And the regulator called its slide on this,
genuinely: `[ARCHIVE TAG]` "Extraordinary Achievements of India."

`[CUT]` Which is a fair title. It's just not the one the rest of the internet chose.

**[FRAME 09_dial]**
Here's what the rest of the internet chose. A 2021 study in PNAS looked at 2.73 million posts and found that every word about
the "other side" raised the odds of a share by about 67%. Outrage at an out-group isn't a bug in the feed. It's the fuel.

**[FRAME 10_angry]** `[SFX: coins dropped on wood, one, then five]`
And for years Facebook made that explicit. According to its own internal documents, reported by the Washington Post, from 2017
the feed counted an angry reaction as worth five likes. The weight was later cut, and reached zero by the end of 2020.

`[CUT]` For three years, the most profitable emotion on the platform was "how dare they".

To be fair, critics point out that "angry" was the least-used reaction. It was just the best paid.

**[FRAME 11_repost]**
So here's the business model. Someone posts an ordinary video of a market street. An account somewhere else reposts it with a
mocking caption. Since October 2024, X has paid creators based on engagement from its Premium users.

`[CUT]` Picture it: the original gets a few hundred views. The insult gets a salary. *(The posts on screen are drawn as an illustration, not a real case.)*

**[FRAME 16_fakes]** `[SFX: rubber stamp, three times]`
And some of the "proof" isn't even proof. Fact-checkers have caught viral "disgusting Indian food" clips that were AI-generated,
others that were scripted, and one that wasn't filmed in India at all.

## 4 · STAKES (5:30–7:20)

**[FRAME 12_count]**
CSOH's latest report counted about 542,360 anti-South Asian posts on X in 18 months. In 45% of them, South Asians were "invaders"
or "job thieves". About 30% used a single slur that started on 4chan in 2015. And the fastest-growing kind of insult, up 171%,
was about hygiene.

`[CUT]` The drain inspector has gone digital. And he's got a blue tick.

**[FRAME 13_timing → bars_a/b/c]**
Then it left the screen. In the same months:
- Gardaí in Dublin recorded an assault on an Indian man as racially motivated.
- Rally flyers in Australia singled out Indian migration.
- In Canada, police-reported hate crimes against South Asians went from 324 in 2024 to 483 in 2025, up 49% in a year.

To be precise: no study shows that a post caused an attack. What we can show is the timing.

**[FRAME 14_classifier]**
And moderation has been playing catch-up for years. In 2019 a Facebook test account in Kerala was flooded with hate within three
weeks, and much of it was never flagged, because there were no classifiers for Hindi or Bengali. Facebook says it added them in
2018 and 2020. And of the 128 posts CSOH logged in January 2025, 125 were still up.

`[CUT]` Moderation status: pending. Since 2019.

**[FRAME 15_drains]** *(the other side)*
Now, in fairness, because this channel doesn't do propaganda in either direction: the problems are real, and India's own agencies
count them.
- **Toilets:** 19% of households had no toilet in the 2019–21 national survey.
- **Air:** India ranks 6th of 143 countries for polluted air. ⚠
- **Food:** about one in five food samples failed a standard, though failing a label rule isn't the same as being unsafe.
- **Scams:** scam call centres exist, and Indian police raid them. Indians are also the most targeted: in Microsoft's 2021 survey,
  69% had met a tech-support scam in a year, the highest of any country surveyed.

`[CUT]` So yes, the drains exist. The problem is a feed that only ever shows the drains.

## 5 · SYNTHESIS (7:20–9:00)

**[FRAME 17_sowhat]** `[SFX: three single piano-lid knocks]`
So. What. Now.

**[FRAMES 18_softpower → 18b_achievers]**
First, show the rest. "Naatu Naatu" won an Oscar in 2023. Kalpana Chawla flew to space in 1997. India's payments system now
handles billions of transactions a month.

`[CUT]` Extraordinary achievements, as the regulator would put it. Very modestly.

**[FRAME 18c_faces]**
Second, look at the people. Here are a dozen Indians. None of them went viral. The algorithm looked at them and decided they
weren't disgusting enough.

**[FRAMES rivers → relief_a/b/c]**
And look at the place: the Ganga running down to the Sundarbans, the largest mangrove forest on Earth. A country that overlaps
four of the world's 36 biodiversity hotspots.

**[FRAME 19_notinterested]** `[SFX: one heavy thunk]`
Third, and this one's on us. The feed serves what you keep eating. When it hands you a clip built to make you feel superior,
there's a button for that. It's called "Not interested". It's the cheapest act of good manners on the internet.

**[FRAME 20_end]** `[SFX: old TV switching off]`
India isn't a caricature. It's a country of about 1.4 billion people that the feed keeps editing down to one drain. And laughing
at that edit is a joke that hasn't been funny since 1857.

next frame. The timings get measured against the voice once it exists.

## Rules this draft keeps
- **Satire targets** systems, documents and official language: the Company, a 1835 *Minute*, ranking weights, a slide title, a
  moderation queue. No party, and no named person's character.
- **No John Oliver catchphrases or bits.**
- **Real people:** shown only in credited photos, framed neutrally; nothing drawn on a face. Hateful captions are blacked out.
- **No copyrighted clips,** film footage or news footage.
- **Timing, not cause,** for the offline incidents.
- **The other side** is shown in full (15_drains, 16_fakes).

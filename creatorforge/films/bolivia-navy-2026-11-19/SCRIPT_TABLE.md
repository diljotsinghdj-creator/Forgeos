# Why Does Landlocked Bolivia Have a Navy? Script, draft 1 (four-column)

- **Channel:** Print Facts, long form, 16:9, about 9–10 minutes. Non-partisan; no living politician is named.
- **Engine:** A, *Reverse Autopsy* (owner's pick, 2026-11-19, recorded in `pick.json`). Open on the navy at ~3,800 m, rewind
  date by date to the day the coast went missing (14 Feb 1879), then play forward to today (1904 treaty → 2013–2018 ICJ → Día del Mar).
- **Structure and voice:** the Vox × Johnny Harris five beats, in the John Oliver voice: fact → receipt → simile → snap back.
  Faceless narrator speaking as "I" (the investigator). Written for motion (MASTER_DIRECTIVES §10b).
- **The reverse-time device:** a crimson date stamp sits top-left all film. Act 2 rewinds it (`2026 ◀◀ 1963 ◀◀ 1904 ◀◀ 1884 ◀◀ 1879 ◀◀ 1878`)
  with a tape-rewind foley and a clack on each stop; act 3 flips it to `▶▶` and plays forward to `TODAY`.
- **Canvas backbone:** a relief model of the Andes (real terrain: SRTM / Natural Earth, height exaggeration stated in the source line)
  on a cartographer's light table, lit from below. **The coastline of the old Litoral department is a separate cream paper strip
  with a Pacific-teal edge.** It lifts at the 14 Feb 1879 stop, peels off at the end of the war, is laid *beside* the model, and
  stirs in a draught at the end. Every document (treaty, ledger, truce, constitution, ICJ page) slides onto the same light table;
  hard cuts are comedy beats only.
- **Palette:** cream #F4EFE6, charcoal #1A1A1A, crimson #D93829 for evidence (circles, pins, red string, the date stamp), yellow
  #F7D046 for highlighter sweeps only. **Film accent: Pacific teal #2C7A8C**, used only for water and the missing sea: the coastline
  strip's edge, the rivers, the Boliviamar bracket and the "MAR" tag. It is the one thing the film is about and the one thing
  Bolivia doesn't have, so it is the only colour allowed to mean "sea". It is greener than Dragon Psychology's cobalt #2B6CB0, so
  the channels don't blur.
- **Callbacks:**
  - "Look at the altitude" / "Most navies measure depth" (row 1–3) → paid off in the last three rows.
  - "Absolute and perpetual" (Art. II, act 1) → the treaty "uses forever twice" (Art. VI, act 3) → the constitution's
    "irrenunciable e imprescriptible" answers it (act 4) → "a treaty that says forever twice" (act 5).
  - The 23 March pin planted at Topáter (act 3) → Día del Mar (act 4) → the last line (act 5).
  - "Ten centavos" (act 2) → returns in the synthesis receipts row.
- **Evidence first (gate 3):** the first explanation (the lake's altitude, why there is no coast) comes *after* a real document:
  **the 1904 Treaty of Peace and Friendship, Article II**, row 4, with "dominio absoluto y perpetuo de Chile" highlighted.
  - **Preferred scan:** the treaty as printed in *Papers Relating to the Foreign Relations of the United States, 1905*
    (US government publication, public domain), digitised by the University of Wisconsin Digital Collections
    (search.library.wisc.edu, FRUS 1905) and HathiTrust; text page at history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1905 ⚠ (confirm the
    exact document number; search suggests d128). This gives the English text; for the Spanish line on screen,
    **use a scan of the Spanish original** from the *Diario Oficial de la República de Chile*, 27 Mar 1905 (public domain by age) ⚠
    to locate, or Bolivia's ICJ Memorial Annex 106 ⚠ licence to check.
  - **Fallback:** a typeset facsimile from the Spanish text (efe.cl PDF) is *not* a real document for gate 3, so if no scan clears,
    the receipt becomes the ICJ judgment's first page (api.icj-cij.org, 153-20181001-JUD-01-00-EN.pdf) and the owner is told.
  - The row 1 boat is an AI plate (illustration, not evidence). If a licensed real photo of the Bolivian navy on Titicaca is found
    (Wikimedia Commons "Bolivian Navy" category ⚠ not verified), it replaces AI·1 and becomes the first receipt.
- **Satire targets:** treaty prose, ceremony bureaucracy, court language and geography. Never Bolivia, Bolivians, Chileans, Abaroa, or
  any living politician. Abaroa's death is told straight, with no joke near it.
- **The other side:** Chile's position gets its own card (valid, ratified, transit honoured; the ICJ agreed there is no duty to
  negotiate); Bolivia's gets its own (signed after defeat, unjust; claim "irrenunciable"). The 1874 tax clause is shown as Chile's
  argument next to Bolivia's sovereign-tax argument. The navy's practical work (rivers, smuggling, floods) is shown before the
  symbolic reading, and the symbolic reading is labelled `OUR READING`.
- **Maps:** all drawn by the studio from real geodata (Natural Earth admin-0, rivers and lakes; SRTM relief). The 1879 boundary and
  the Litoral strip are drawn from a historical boundary source ⚠ (to pick: e.g. the 1866/1874 treaty lines as published; no AI).
- **Facts:** every figure is in `dossier.json` with its source and status. ⚠ marks an item to check before voice.
- **Bracket notes:**
  - `[CUT]`: a 0.2 s music cut before a punchline, the only allowed hard cut.
  - `[HOLD 0.6s]`: a pause after a reveal.
  - `[AI·n]`: an AI plate (flat paper collage, labelled AI ILLUSTRATION, no faces, never a map, chart, number or evidence). Listed at the end.
  - **Shot 1 / Shot 2:** a row split into two shots for the pacing gate.
- **Status:** draft 1, for the owner's approval. **No video until "go"**; the owner chooses the voice.

December 1954. A scientist is taking secret notes in a stranger's house. The people around him are waiting for a flying saucer. He's waiting for it not to come. He isn't a believer. He's a researcher. Undercover. And he's not the only spy in this group. What they wrote down became the founding case of a phrase you know. Cognitive dissonance. Dorothy Martin had predicted a flood for 21 December. North America would go under. Believers would be picked up the day before. Boarding rules. Remove all metal. Bring a stamped envelope as a passport. And the password is "I left my hat at home." Interstellar travel. Still gated by a hat-check desk. That's how the 1956 book tells it. You'd have believed the scientists too. They were in the room. A 2025 paper says one of them did more than take notes.

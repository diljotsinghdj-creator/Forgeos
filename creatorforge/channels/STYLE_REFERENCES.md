# Style references from the owner (2026-11-12/13)

The owner's own picks for how our films should look. Study these before designing any film, sample or still, and keep
innovating inside them. Sources: a Pinterest "vox media" board (screenshot) and a 3-minute screen recording of
collage explainers (both shown in session, 2026-11-13).

## What every reference shares
- **Collage, not graphics.** Real photographs cut out in black-and-white halftone, on paper, with torn edges, tape,
  stamps and scraps. Colour comes from flat paper shapes and one spot colour, not from the photos.
- **Motion builds the frame.** Pieces drop, slide and stamp in one by one; the camera drifts or tilts in 3D; nothing
  appears all at once.
- **Captions burned in word by word**: bold white uppercase, the spoken word in a blue (or yellow) box. Often vertical.
- **One idea per frame**, readable in a second.

## Deep study of the screen recording (2026-11-13, frame by frame at 3 fps)

### How the pictures are made
- **The Swiss-banking and Reliance pieces are AI-generated stills, then animated.** The tells: photoreal tabletop
  lighting that no stock photo has (parcels, wax seals, a lifted paper Switzerland casting a real shadow), gibberish
  print in the collage ("Fontery Vilusatted", "Kaaie AM", "Business 21 ... Minutions"), and the board's own pin
  "AI documentary, Vox style tutorial: Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini Omni Flash". The workflow: generate one finished frame
  per beat, cut it into layers (or generate the empty base, then the same image with each prop added), and build
  it up in After Effects.
- **The surreal flat-colour pieces (Entroz, Zahin Nahian) are real archival photos**: B&W cut-outs, relief-map pieces
  with rocky 3D texture, a pot, a broom, passports. Private people's eyes are covered with a white strip.
- **The kinetic pieces (Halosta)** are stock photos and type on a white grid paper with a vignette.

### How it moves (the part we lacked)
1. **The build.** A frame is never shown finished. It starts empty (a ledger, a map) and fills one element per beat,
   about every 0.5-1 s: logo, then a row of photos, then a red X scribbled across them, then the hero object, then the
   headline sticker. The frame ends dense.
2. **Pop with overshoot.** Every element scales in from about 80-90% to 105% and settles, or drops in with a shadow.
   A building flies in with heavy motion blur. A refinery grows up from the bottom edge.
3. **Elements lift off the base.** The country is a separate, thicker paper piece that drops onto the map and glows.
   Then arrows slide in from all sides on a short stagger, parcels march in along a line, red string draws itself,
   and a stamp lands.
4. **Torn-paper and piece transitions.** The map tears into puzzle pieces between scenes, and red torn paper wipes
   the frame.
5. **The camera is always moving.** A slow push or tilt on the map, 2.5D parallax (clouds and ships drifting over a
   vintage map), and whip moves with directional motion blur between "pages" in the kinetic style.
6. **Kinetic type.** Words arrive one by one with motion blur and a slight 3D tilt. One key word or number is set big
   in blue ("40 years of your life"). Depth of field keeps foreground objects soft.
7. **Hand-made jitter.** Doodles (sun, lightning, scribbled X) and ransom-note letters boil at about 12 fps. The
   ransom letters appear one at a time on a torn red paper blob ("OPTIMIZE it").
8. **Captions.** Bold white uppercase, 2-6 words, low-centre. The spoken word sits in a blue box (sometimes yellow),
   moving word by word.
9. **Texture.** Film grain, vignette, paper stains; flat colour grounds have noise.

### What this means for our studio
- Already have: real B&W cut-outs, map desk, torn paper, stamps, red string, highlighter, 12 fps stepped motion.
- Must add: the progressive build (one element per beat), pop-and-settle on every piece, motion-blur whips, the
  blue-box word captions, ransom-note letters, a boiling-doodle layer, an eye-strip for private people in archive
  photos, puzzle and torn-paper transitions, parallax drift on maps.
- The owner's decision: whether to use AI-generated scene plates (props and tabletops, never real people's faces)
  the way these creators do. Owner's rule: no images are created without asking.

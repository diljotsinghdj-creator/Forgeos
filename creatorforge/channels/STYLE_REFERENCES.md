# Style references from the owner (2026-11-12/13)

The owner's own picks for how our films should look. Study these before designing any film, sample or still, and keep
innovating inside them. Sources: a Pinterest "vox media" board (screenshot) and a 3-minute screen recording of
collage explainers (both shown in session, 2026-11-13).

## What every reference shares
- **Collage, not graphics.** Real photographs cut out in black-and-white halftone, on paper, with torn edges, tape,
  stamps and scraps. Colour comes from flat paper shapes and one spot colour, not from the photos.
- **Motion builds the frame.** Pieces drop, slide and stamp in one by one; the camera drifts or tilts in 3D; nothing
  appears all at once.
- **Captions burned in word by word**: bold white uppercase, the spoken word in a blue (or yellow) box. Often vertical.
- **One idea per frame**, readable in a second.

## The styles (each to be rotated and pushed further, never copied)
| Style | Ground | Signature moves |
|---|---|---|
| A. Map desk | aged paper map, full frame | country glowing red; black paper arrows converging; red string from pinned photos; props on the map (gold bars, banknotes, rubber stamps, vault, parcels); CONFIDENTIAL / ARCHIVE stamps; clouds and ships drifting; slow 3D camera |
| B. Scrapbook build | ledger / newsprint | dense torn halftone photos, yellow-red-black paper scraps, torn sticker labels with condensed headlines, a logo, pieces added until the frame is full |
| C. Surreal cut-out on flat colour | bold flat green / yellow / red, or grey concrete paper | one surreal B&W combination (a map as a head, a statue on a tank), thin geometric outline, a few props, ransom-note letters |
| D. Clean grid paper | light grid paper | kinetic type with motion blur, real objects, dashed circles, 3D tilt |
| E. Editorial poster (the board) | cream paper or one flat colour | huge B&W cut-out breaking into huge type, flat disc / burst / torn block, one spot-colour piece, doodles |
| F. Paper-map diorama | folded paper map | landmarks rising in 3D out of the map |

## Deep study of the screen recording (2026-11-13, frame by frame at 3 fps)

### How the pictures are made
- **The Swiss-banking and Reliance pieces are AI-generated stills, then animated.** The tells: photoreal tabletop
  lighting that no stock photo has (parcels, wax seals, a lifted paper Switzerland casting a real shadow), gibberish
  print in the collage ("Fontery Vilusatted", "Kaaie AM", "Business 21 ... Minutions"), and the board's own pin
  "AI documentary, Vox style tutorial: Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini Omni Flash". The workflow: generate one finished frame
  per beat, cut it into layers (or generate the empty base, then the same image with each prop added), and build
  it up in After Effects.
- **The surreal flat-colour pieces (Entroz, Zahin Nahian) are real archival photos**: B&W cut-outs, relief-map pieces
  with rocky 3D texture, a pot, a broom, passports. Private people's eyes are covered with a white strip.
- **The kinetic pieces (Halosta)** are stock photos and type on a white grid paper with a vignette.

### How it moves (the part we lacked)
1. **The build.** A frame is never shown finished. It starts empty (a ledger, a map) and fills one element per beat,
   about every 0.5-1 s: logo, then a row of photos, then a red X scribbled across them, then the hero object, then the
   headline sticker. The frame ends dense.
2. **Pop with overshoot.** Every element scales in from about 80-90% to 105% and settles, or drops in with a shadow.
   A building flies in with heavy motion blur. A refinery grows up from the bottom edge.
3. **Elements lift off the base.** The country is a separate, thicker paper piece that drops onto the map and glows.
   Then arrows slide in from all sides on a short stagger, parcels march in along a line, red string draws itself,
   and a stamp lands.
4. **Torn-paper and piece transitions.** The map tears into puzzle pieces between scenes, and red torn paper wipes
   the frame.
5. **The camera is always moving.** A slow push or tilt on the map, 2.5D parallax (clouds and ships drifting over a
   vintage map), and whip moves with directional motion blur between "pages" in the kinetic style.
6. **Kinetic type.** Words arrive one by one with motion blur and a slight 3D tilt. One key word or number is set big
   in blue ("40 years of your life"). Depth of field keeps foreground objects soft.
7. **Hand-made jitter.** Doodles (sun, lightning, scribbled X) and ransom-note letters boil at about 12 fps. The
   ransom letters appear one at a time on a torn red paper blob ("OPTIMIZE it").
8. **Captions.** Bold white uppercase, 2-6 words, low-centre. The spoken word sits in a blue box (sometimes yellow),
   moving word by word.
9. **Texture.** Film grain, vignette, paper stains; flat colour grounds have noise.

### What this means for our studio
- Already have: real B&W cut-outs, map desk, torn paper, stamps, red string, highlighter, 12 fps stepped motion.
- Must add: the progressive build (one element per beat), pop-and-settle on every piece, motion-blur whips, the
  blue-box word captions, ransom-note letters, a boiling-doodle layer, an eye-strip for private people in archive
  photos, puzzle and torn-paper transitions, parallax drift on maps.
- The owner's decision: whether to use AI-generated scene plates (props and tabletops, never real people's faces)
  the way these creators do. Owner's rule: no images are created without asking.

## Articles studied (owner's links, 2026-11-13)

### PremiumBeat: 5 breakdowns on replicating the Vox motion-graphic look (Lewis McGregor, 2021)
1. **12 fps graphics in a 24 fps edit.** Animate the graphics composition at 12 fps and drop it into 24 fps: the
   stutter gives plain elements character. (Studio: done, stepped 12 fps.)
2. **Tracking transition.** Two shots as 3D layers; a 3D camera moves backward across the cut on eased keyframes whose
   speed peaks exactly on the edit point; an adjustment-layer blur ramps up to the cut and down after, much shorter
   than the camera move. (Studio: to build, "track".)
3. **Step-by-step lower third.** A rough-edged texture revealed by a mask path grown frame by frame in a jagged way,
   skipping frames; the text revealed the same way a few frames later. (Studio: to build, jagged reveal.)
4. **Motion background.** Several paper/grunge textures swapped two or three times a second behind a still photo, so
   a long hold is never dead. (Studio: grain swap every 10 frames; make the paper itself swap.)
5. **Lens characteristics.** Chromatic aberration and a Gaussian blur of 3.5, only at the edges (centre mask
   subtracted, feather 50). (Studio: done 2026-11-13, edge-only.)

### Easy-Peasy.AI: how to make Vox-style videos with AI (2026)
- **Script formula** (Johnny Harris: "visual evidence, then context"): cold open on a visual anchor -> one question ->
  short context bridges -> the anchor returns -> zoom out to the bigger idea. Music changes about every 20 s, ducked,
  animation cut to the beat. Lines written to be spoken ("Look at this", "Here's the thing").
- **Seven signatures:** kinetic type with the yellow highlight sweep; archival photos as paper cut-outs with
  parallax; zooming annotated maps (lat, lon, zoom, bearing, pitch; offset bearing so the camera comes down and
  orbits); data that answers the narrator on the number; 12 fps; texture on everything (no pure white); muted palette
  with one loud accent.
- **The image-first pipeline:** lock one style frame, generate every scene still from it (image edit with the style
  frame as reference: "Using the exact same style, palette and textures as this reference image, create a new scene
  showing..."), then animate each still with image-to-video. Reuse the style sentence word for word.
- One narration line of at most 20 words and one visual idea per scene; name the imperfections in prompts (grain,
  paper, hand-drawn, choppy); never let a model invent statistics.

### Kapwing: how to make informational videos like Vox (2026)
- Beats of 2-3 s (5-8 spoken words), one visual idea each; a 2-minute script made 51 beats.
- One finished collage image per beat, then an image-to-video pass that BUILDS TOWARD it. Their animation rule:
  locked camera; 0-7 s the frame opens on the empty plate and elements enter one by one, back to front, in narrative
  order (background scraps, the hero cut-out slides in with paper drag and a small settle, supporting cut-outs drop or
  pin with a 2-frame stamp settle, tape, typewriter strips, stamps, red string pin to pin, marker lines last); each
  lands with a small bounce and a real shadow and never moves again; 7-10 s a "living poster" (corners lift, halftone
  shimmers, shadows breathe). Audio: paper sounds only (slide, tap, tape, stamp thud, string zip, pin click).
- Edit: trim each clip to the beat, simple cuts (the paper motion is the movement), music, paper sound effects,
  subtitles.

### Digi Curator: viral Vox-style animations for free (2026)
- Thin article (its prompts are behind a sign-up). Useful: the short-form "archived photo" treatment: an old
  black-and-white portrait pops off the page with a red cut-out outline and a small bounce, a stamped label
  (ARCHIVED / CLASSIFIED) lands on it, and a Ken Burns push in or out runs under the voiceover.
- Consistency trick: ONE universal motion prompt applied to every image in the film, so every cut moves the same way
  (the collage-explainer skill already does this with a verbatim style block; add a verbatim motion block too).
- Pipeline: a chatbot writes the script, beats and one image prompt per beat; images are batch-generated (they use a
  Chrome extension on Google Flow; we use ElevenLabs flows); one motion prompt animates them all.
- For us: the archived-photo pop needs no AI. The studio already has real cut-outs, the red offset print and stamps;
  a person in it must be a real credited photo (never generated).

### YouTube kg_N3dJ8cCs: "How to create videos like Vox with Claude" (owner-sent, 2026-11-13)
Studied from the transcript only (the visual walkthrough was refused: vidIQ credits ran out). A Claude Code +
HyperFrames (HTML-to-video, like our Chromium capture in vox.py) tutorial. Its thesis: the Vox look is three
signatures, then a calm edit.
1. **The highlighted document.** An off-white article page, tilted a little, paper texture so it reads as a real
   printout; a slow push-in while a yellow highlighter sweeps the one key line. "The highlight tells viewers
   exactly what to read." Studio: `Document:` / `Article:` / `Clipping:` (signature #FFDD33 sweep).
2. **The annotated chart.** Light background, faint grid lines, ONE bold line that draws itself, and a small note
   pointing at the part that matters. "The chart explains itself." Numbers go in the prompt so the chart is exact.
   Studio: `Chart:`/line charts with `note:`.
3. **Footage framed like a paper collage**, not filling the screen: a card on the page, a small yellow label, a slow
   zoom. "Framing stock footage makes it look designed, not borrowed." Studio: `Footage: ... | frame: taped`.
4. **Edit:** narration calm and curious, everything cut to the voice, quiet music underneath.
Its demo film (coffee prices; figures theirs, not checked by us) runs: hook line on a cup, the doubled price on a
chart, the cause on framed footage, the consequence, a soft question to end.

**Self-check against our last sample ("Read the whole number"):** it used none of signature 1 and missed 3.
- The study itself was the evidence and was never shown: the move was the paper's title page with the key finding
  highlighted (Thomas & Morwitz 2005, Journal of Consumer Research; the NBER auction paper), not a bare "2005".
- The AI clips ran full-bleed; framed as cards with a yellow label they would sit in the same paper world as the
  data shots, which was the film's biggest style gap.
- The two counters should have been one annotated chart (the price drop at each 10,000-mile mark, a note on the
  cliff).
**Rule from this:** every explainer, either channel, carries at least one highlighted real document, and its numbers
go on an annotated chart rather than a lone counter where a shape exists; AI or stock footage is framed unless it is
the hero shot.

### YouTube RaxX_Q7Apj0: "Vox-style animations with free AI tools" (owner-sent, 2026-11-13)
Studied from the transcript (vidIQ visual credits exhausted). The same pipeline as the Digi Curator article (master
prompt -> niche -> 10 ideas -> script -> visual beats -> one image prompt per beat -> batch Nano Banana stills on
Google Flow -> one universal motion prompt -> clips -> voice -> music -> CapCut). New or sharper points:
- **Beat density:** a 1-minute script became 27 beats, about 2.2 s a picture. Their demo (Victor Lustig "selling" the
  Eiffel Tower, 1925) is told in the present tense, one concrete action per sentence ("Then he boards a train to
  Vienna."), each sentence its own picture. Our 30 s samples ran 8 shots (3.8 s); the reference pace is 12-14.
- **Clip length = cost:** ask the motion model for 5 s, not the 10 s default, at 720p; "the more time an image has to
  animate, the more it costs." Our left-digit clips came back 10 s at 6,105 credits each because the ElevenLabs
  video tool has no duration field: set `duration_secs` on the node (creative_update_node, after
  creative_get_model_schema) or price with estimate_only first. 5 s halves the bill.
- **Omni Flash beat Seedance** in their side-by-side ("much more animated"). They praise Omni for inventing a photo
  that was not in the poster. For us that is a hazard, not a feature: a documentary clip may not add a person,
  document or object the poster did not have. Check every clip for added elements (our clip 2 grew a second ".99"
  scrap) and cut around or reject them.
- **Voice:** ElevenLabs audio tags in square brackets ([whispers], etc.) steer delivery per chunk; generate in chunks
  and audition each. (ElevenLabs only with the owner's permission; Kokoro otherwise.)
- **Music:** instrumental, quiet, "growing intense"; the narration sits well above it (they lift the voice about
  +6.7 dB). Our ducking already does this.
- **Edit:** vary the transitions so they never repeat in a row, mostly slides; skip burned-in captions when the
  pictures already carry text pointers (for us: blue-box captions only on shots without baked text).
- Their story facts are unsourced; nothing from the demo goes into our films without the dossier.
**Applied:** next collage sample: 12-14 beats per 30 s, clips priced first and set to 5 s, every clip checked for
invented elements.

### GitHub Alisa0808/vox-director (owner-sent, 2026-11-13; MIT)
The upstream original of the vox-explainer zip: topic -> beat map (gate) -> theme bake-off (gate) -> one collage
poster per shot (Nano Banana 2) -> Omni Flash motion -> TTS + music -> ffmpeg assembly, all on the paid Atlas Cloud
API. Paid parts dropped (section 9 of the directives); also not for us: Kling for real people, the AI presenter
("host") mode, A-roll restyling of a face, the watermark. Kept, free:
- **Cadence:** shots 3-6 s, never past about 7 s; each beat's narration (8-10 s) carries TWO shots, a wide with the
  headline and a detail cut-in without it, the narration running across the cut mid-sentence.
- **Real things stay real:** a real photo is cut out as a photographic sticker, never redrawn; poses go to the body,
  halftone is kept off skin, labels are frozen (their video model re-lettered "PARFUM" to "PAREUM").
- **The local element engine** (no video model): cut the finished poster into pieces, fly them back to their
  ORIGINAL positions so the assembled frame IS the poster; entrances fly-in with overshoot, slap, drop with bounce,
  pop-settle; settled pieces keep a slow sway; clean each piece's home spot first so no copy shows before it lands.
  **Built as the studio's `Assemble:` kind** (media/assemble.py): automatic split (flat ground + salient mask, cut
  at narrow joins) or hand-picked boxes; spots filled with the surrounding ground colour and grain (their blur fix
  and inpainting both left visible ghosts on busy posters in our tests); a poster that will not split is a slow push.

### GitHub iart-ai/map-animation-skills (owner-sent, 2026-11-13; MIT, free text; the After Effects path is paid, dropped)
"Vox-style" editorial maps: a camera that zooms, orbits or pans over a base map while pins drop, routes draw on,
regions highlight and labels call out. Two paths: Google Earth Studio -> After Effects (dropped: AE is paid) or
code-rendered vector maps (GeoJSON -> SVG with a real projection), which is what the studio already does.
Rules worth keeping, checked against the studio:
- **12 fps stutter on the graphics only**, base camera smooth at 24 ("stuttering everything looks like dropped
  frames"). The studio already does this (vox.py: tracks quantised to 12 fps, TICKS run every frame). Applying it
  caught a bug in the new Assemble kind (its push ran on the 12 fps tracks); fixed, the push is now a TICK.
- **One projection for everything** (basemap, pins, routes, labels in one coordinate space); [lng, lat] order.
- **Great-circle arcs** for flights (densify with geoInterpolate): our Globe does this; flat Route maps use arcs.
- **Focus + context:** the target region lit, the rest dimmed but visible; colour = meaning, never reassigned.
- **Labels:** upright, a couple of words, inside the safe area, counter-scaled so type stays one size while the map
  zooms; they appear after their pin lands.
- **Camera:** slow and deliberate ("establish, don't whip"), eased; fly A->B by zooming out toward the midpoint and
  back in.
- **One ordered beats array** drives camera targets, pin order, routes and labels (our Route/Pinmap lines already).
- **Verify by stills:** frame 0, mid each beat, last; check every pin on its real coordinate and two adjacent frames
  inside a move (the overlay holds for 2 frames while the camera advances).

### Eight Claude/Remotion video repos (owner-sent, 2026-11-13): what to take
Read only, nothing installed. **Remotion itself is not for us:** source-available, free only for individuals and
very small companies, a paid company licence above that, so it fails the free-tools rule (section 9). Our
Playwright + ffmpeg studio already does what it does. Ideas worth rebuilding in our own code:
1. **Audio-aware cut placement** (AgriciDaniel/claude-shorts `scripts/snap_boundaries.py`): snap a cut to a word
   start, prefer a sentence start, extend to finish the sentence if within 3 s, +300 ms pad; silence detection on a
   measured threshold (remotion-dev/skills silence-detection: loudnorm's input_thresh as silencedetect's level).
   For shorts cutdowns and for cuts that land off their line.
2. **Word captions, Vox style**: word timings from our own narration, paged at most 2 lines x 24 characters
   (OpenChatCut caption paging), drawn as a cream strip behind each word then a yellow sweep once all are in
   (faceless-shorts-creator `SerifStatement`), in our signature #FFDD33.
3. **Speed** (Kianzzz/xilo-opus-video, MIT, same architecture as ours): pipe screenshots straight into ffmpeg (no
   PNGs on disk), re-render one segment only. Done today separately: the optical-finish mask is computed once.
4. **SFX as a cue sheet** (hassancs91/claude-youtube-editor `tools/mix_sfx.py`): one cue per effect with its own
   gain, ducked under the voice, a limiter, and a measured check that each cue is audible; the owner approves the
   sheet. (Its bundled ElevenLabs SFX are not reusable.)
5. **More pre-show checks** (OpenChatCut export QA, claude-youtube-editor `scan_flicker.py`): black frames, frozen
   frames, silence, levels, 1-2 frame flicker, reported in PRESHOW.md.
Skipped: editor-pro-max and remotion-video-skill (generic Remotion templates, paid TTS), OpenChatCut's code (AGPL,
paid providers), every ElevenLabs / Gemini / fal path, AI-generated "archival" images.

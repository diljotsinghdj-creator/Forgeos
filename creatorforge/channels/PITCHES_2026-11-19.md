## Film 4 · Print Facts: Why does landlocked Bolivia have a navy?
**Handles:** geography (the lost coast), time (1879 → 1904 → 2018) and numbers (the altitude, the ICJ vote).

**The spine:**
- Bolivia lost its coast in the War of the Pacific (1879–84), and the 1904 treaty with Chile made the loss permanent.
- It kept a navy anyway, which trains on Lake Titicaca, about 3,800 m up ⚠.
- Bolivia marks a "Day of the Sea" every 23 March ⚠.
- In 2018 the International Court of Justice ruled that Chile has no obligation to negotiate sea access (12–3 ⚠).

**Satire target:** a 140-year-old border, official ceremonies and treaty language. No living politician.

## Film 5 · Dragon Psychology: The buttons that do nothing
**Handles:** numbers (how many buttons are dummies), geography (a New York street grid) and time (a 1975 lab study up to today).

**The spine:**
- Many crosswalk buttons in New York were reported to do nothing; the New York Times counted about 2,500 of 3,250 in 2004 ⚠.
- Many lift "door close" buttons don't shorten the wait, partly because of the US disability law's door-timing rules (ADA, 1990 ⚠).
- Ellen Langer's 1975 "illusion of control" studies found that people who picked their own lottery ticket wanted more money to
  sell it than people who were handed one (amounts ⚠).

**Satire target:** the brain as an incompetent middle manager pressing buttons to feel busy. No real person is mocked.

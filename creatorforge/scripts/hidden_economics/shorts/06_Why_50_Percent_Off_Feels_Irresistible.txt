You compared fifty to a hundred, not to zero.

Why is that first number so powerful?
Visual: Extreme close-up of the crossed-out number on the tag, shallow focus, number blurred
Text: WHY THE FIRST NUMBER?

Psychologists call this anchoring, studied by Tversky and Kahneman.
Visual: An old academic journal lying open on a desk under lamp light, text unreadable
Text: ANCHORING · TVERSKY & KAHNEMAN, 1974

The first number drags your judgement towards it.
Visual: A small boat anchor resting on a dark wooden table, a chain trailing out of frame
Text: FIRST NUMBER = ANCHOR

So who chose that anchor?
Visual: Wide shot of a clothing rail in a dim shop, rows of tags dangling, nobody in frame

The seller. And it works even if you never wanted a jacket.
Visual: A hand lifting the jacket off the rail, seen from behind, soft spotlight

So ask, were you looking for a jacket this morning?
Visual: A person from behind standing at a front door in morning light, coat hook empty
Text: WERE YOU LOOKING FOR IT?

If yes, fifty off is a genuine saving.
Visual: Close-up of the jacket folded neatly on a bed, warm morning light
Text: YES → SAVED £50

If no, you did not save fifty. You spent fifty.
Visual: Close-up of a phone banking screen with one payment highlighted, text blurred
Text: NO → SPENT £50

Decide you want it first. Then look at the percentage.
Visual: A hand covering a price tag with one finger while holding the garment up to the light
Text: WANT IT FIRST · PRICE SECOND

Half off something you never wanted is still fifty pounds gone.

Being clever does not protect you from a well-timed phone call.

Text: TOO SMART TO FALL FOR IT?

Scams rarely test your intelligence. They test your timing.
Visual: Close-up of a wall clock above a cluttered desk, evening light

Imagine you are tired, rushing, and expecting a delivery.
Visual: Silhouette of a person from behind carrying shopping bags through a doorway
Stock: person carrying shopping bags

A text arrives about that delivery, at exactly the wrong moment.
Visual: Extreme close-up of a phone notification glow on a kitchen counter, text unreadable
Text: FICTIONAL MESSAGE

Then a caller asks you to read back a code you just received.
Visual: Macro shot of a phone screen showing a blurred six-digit code
Text: "JUST READ ME THE CODE"

That code is often the key to your account.
Visual: A single brass key on a dark table, sharp spotlight

And feeling sure you would spot it can stop you checking.
Visual: A hand waving away a phone on a desk, motion blur

So the safest habit is not being smarter. It is a rule.
Visual: Wide shot of a tidy desk with a single notebook, calm warm light

Never share a one-time code with anyone who contacts you.
Visual: Close-up of a thumb covering a phone screen, screen details unreadable
Text: NEVER SHARE A ONE-TIME CODE

Stop, challenge, and verify through a channel you choose yourself.
Visual: Hands holding a phone to an ear by a window in morning light
Text: STOP. CHALLENGE. PROTECT.

Cleverness will not save you from that call. That rule will.

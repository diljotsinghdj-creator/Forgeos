Title: The Fear-Based Sales Trick: Problem First, Product Second
Tags: #HiddenEconomics #fearmarketing #advertising #consumerpsychology #criticalthinking

Watch the order. The advert scares you first, then offers to save you.
Visual: Dark hallway at night, a front door with a faint gap of light beneath it
Text: SCARED FIRST. SOLD SECOND.

Picture a made-up advert. A shadow at the window, a worried face.
Visual: Silhouette behind frosted glass, cold blue light, no face visible
Text: FICTIONAL ADVERT

Then a calm voice and a product at a special price.
Visual: Box on a bright white table, generic packaging, no brand or text
Text: ILLUSTRATION

Some risks are real, and protection can be worth paying for.
Visual: Close-up of a hand turning a deadbolt on a wooden door, warm light
Stock: locking front door

The problem is timing. Fear arrives before the facts do.
Visual: Extreme close-up of a racing pulse on a wrist, shallow focus

Why does that matter? Because anxiety makes urgency feel like good sense.
Visual: Hand gripping a phone tightly, thumb near a bright button, text unreadable

So checking the price or alternatives starts to feel like a delay.
Visual: Price comparison sheet pushed aside on a cluttered desk, words unreadable

The fix is two calm questions.
Visual: Wide shot of a quiet kitchen table, notebook and pen, morning light

How likely is this danger, really?
Visual: Hand drawing a small dot on a large blank page, desk lamp glow
Text: HOW LIKELY IS IT?

And what does this product actually do about it?
Visual: Hands turning the generic box over to read the back, text unreadable
Text: WHAT DOES IT ACTUALLY DO?

If the advert can't answer both, the fear was doing the selling.
Visual: The dark hallway again, a lamp now lit, the door ordinary

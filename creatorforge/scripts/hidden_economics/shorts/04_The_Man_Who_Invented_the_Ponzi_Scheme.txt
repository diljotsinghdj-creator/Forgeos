A Boston man promised fifty percent profit in forty-five days.
Visual: A tiny paper postal coupon pinned beneath a towering stack of old banknotes, cut out and pasted on yellowed paper
Text: BOSTON, 1920

Hand him a hundred dollars, get one fifty back.
Visual: Two old banknotes paper-clipped to a handwritten promissory note on a desk, ink text unreadable
Chart: $100 → $150 | promised in 45 days (illustration)

His secret, he claimed, was postal coupons bought cheaply in Europe.
Visual: An old map of the Atlantic with red string running from Europe to Boston, small paper coupons taped along it

So why had so few coupons ever been cashed?
Visual: An empty wooden post office drawer under a single desk lamp, a red circle drawn around it
Text: WHERE WERE THE COUPONS?

Because early investors were paid with newer investors' money.
Visual: Hands passing banknotes from one envelope to the next along a long row of open envelopes
Text: OLD INVESTORS PAID WITH NEW MONEY

Roughly fifteen million dollars poured in within about eight months.
Visual: Piles of old banknotes and letters overflowing a wooden office desk
Chart: $15,000,000 | taken in over about 8 months

Then reporters started asking questions, and the payouts stopped.
Visual: A 1920s newspaper taped to a desk, a red-pen circle around a headline, text unreadable

By August nineteen twenty, Ponzi was under arrest.

Fifty percent in forty-five days was never profit. Just someone else's deposit.

The Small Yes That Leads to a Big Yes
On-screen hook: TINY YES → HUGE YES
Title: 17% Said Yes Cold, 76% After a Tiny Favour: The Small Yes Trick
Tags: #HiddenEconomics #psychology #persuasion #sales #scams
Source: Freedman, J. L. and Fraser, S. C. (1966), Compliance without pressure, the foot-in-the-door technique, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology 4(2), https://doi.org/10.1037/h0023552; Foot-in-the-door technique overview, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foot-in-the-door_technique

A huge, ugly sign on their lawn? Most homeowners said no.
Visual: A giant crooked wooden sign almost hiding a small suburban house, cut out and pasted on paper, red string tied round it
Text: STANFORD RESEARCHERS, 1966

Asked cold, only about seventeen percent agreed.
Visual: A row of paper house cut-outs on a desk, nearly all of them crossed out in red pen
Chart: 17% | agreed when asked cold

Then one tiny favour, weeks earlier, changed the answer.
Visual: Extreme close-up of a small square card propped in a window, its lettering unreadable
Text: WHAT FAVOUR?

A small sign in the window, about safe driving.
Visual: A modest house window with a tiny card in the corner, afternoon light
Text: "BE A SAFE DRIVER"
Stock: 1960s suburban houses

Weeks later, about seventy-six percent said yes to the giant one.
Visual: The same paper houses, now most of them circled in red
Chart: 76% vs 17% | small yes first, asked cold

Saying yes once changes how you see yourself.
Visual: A hand signing a small card, its shadow on the wall far larger than the hand
Text: "I'M SOMEONE WHO HELPS"

Sales funnels often start the same way, with a free quiz or tiny trial.

Title: The Missing Stop Button: Who Chose The Next Episode?
Tags: #HiddenEconomics #autoplay #bingewatching #attention #screentime

Five. Four. Three. The next episode starts before you've decided anything.
Visual: Dark living room, a television glowing with a generic countdown circle, text unreadable
Text: 5... 4... 3...

So who actually chose to keep watching?
Visual: Close-up of a remote control resting untouched on a sofa arm, blue screen light

That's autoplay. It makes continuing effortless.
Visual: Wide shot from behind a silhouette on a sofa, screen light flickering
Stock: watching tv dark room

Sometimes that's genuinely convenient. Halfway through a series, it saves a click.
Visual: Hand reaching into a bowl of popcorn, warm lamp beside the sofa

But notice what flipped. Stopping now needs an action. Continuing needs none.
Visual: Extreme close-up of a thumb hovering above a remote's buttons, not pressing
Text: STOP = ACTION · CONTINUE = NOTHING

The decision is made by default, not deliberately.
Visual: Clock on a dark shelf, hands moving past midnight
Stock: clock midnight

Many streaming and video apps let you switch autoplay off in settings.
Visual: Phone showing a generic settings page with one toggle sliding off, text unreadable
Text: FICTIONAL INTERFACE

Try it for a week, and watch what happens to your evenings.
Visual: Paper calendar on a fridge, seven days marked in pencil
Text: 7 DAYS

Every next episode becomes a choice you actually make.
Visual: Thumb pressing a single play button on a remote, deliberate and slow

Some nights you'll press play. Some nights you'll go to bed.
Visual: Television switching off, the room falling into darkness

Either way, three, two, one is no longer deciding for you.
Visual: The dark screen reflecting a lamp being switched on
Text: YOUR CHOICE

PHONE SHOOT LIST - SHORTS 01 TO 12
Simple faceless shots you can film at home on a phone, with props. Each one replaces the picture(s) for the
spoken line quoted. Film vertical (9:16), steady (rest the phone on a stack of books or use a cheap tripod),
daylight from a window, hands only - no faces, no readable card numbers, names or bank details (cover them
with a sticky note or blur later). Each clip about 3 s unless noted. A clip replaces that line's Visual:
pictures only; any Chart:/Steps:/Globe: on the line stays as it is. Lines with a Screen: or Statement: key are
not listed - the engine draws those full-frame.

SHORT 01 - The Subscription Trap
  1. "You quit in January. It still charged you."
     Top-down, 3 s: hand tears the January page off a paper calendar and drops it on the kitchen table.
  2. "Twelve pounds a month. By June, seventy-two gone."
     Top-down, 4 s: hand places six small stacks of coins in a row, one by one.
  3. "So why did nobody notice?"
     Close, 3 s: a pile of unopened envelopes on a hallway shelf, slow push in.
  4. "Find payments that repeat, month after month."
     Top-down, 3 s: a red pen circles the same line on three printed statements (text blurred or covered).
  5. "Ask of each one, would I buy this today?"
     Close, 3 s: hand holds a phone face down, then turns it over (screen dark), on the table.
  6. "If not, cancel it before June comes round again."
     Close, 3 s: green pen circles June on the wall calendar.

SHORT 02 - The Free Trial That Is Not Really Free
  1. "It said free. Day eight: ten pounds gone."
     Close, 3 s: red pen circles the 8th on a paper calendar.
  2. "Why did a free trial need your card?"
     Close, 3 s: bank card (number covered by a sticky note) held between two fingers above a desk.
  3. "Because the trial is not the product. The renewal is."
     Top-down, 3 s: magnifying glass slides over the small print at the bottom of any letter (blurred).
  4. "Day seven passes quietly, because nothing asks you to decide."
     Low and close, 3 s: a few envelopes dropping onto a doormat; or a phone face down on a sofa cushion.
  5. "Forget it for a year, and what does free cost?"
     Top-down, 4 s: hand lays twelve coins in a long row across a wooden table.
  6. "Before tapping start, read the renewal price. Set a day-six reminder."
     Close, 3 s: hands set an alarm clock (or a kitchen timer) on the table.
  7. "Then day eight costs only what you chose."
     Close, 3 s: green pen ticks the 8th on the calendar.

SHORT 03 - Where Did the Cancel Button Go
  1. "One tap in. Five taps out. Why?"
     Top-down, 3 s: five sticky notes stuck in a row, the hand adds the last one in red.
  2. "Settings, account, manage plan, then a discount to stay."
     Top-down, 3 s: finger taps along a hand-drawn paper flowchart of five boxes.
  3. "Tap five, in tiny grey letters, finally lets you go."
     Macro/close, 3 s: fingertip hovers over a tiny pencil word written in a page corner.
  4. "Clumsy design, or something common?"
     Top-down, 3 s: hands sketching phone-shaped boxes on paper.
  5. "Possible, not proven illegal. So where is the line?"
     Close, 3 s: a ruler laid down slowly across a closed thick book.
  6. "Checking it is really you is fair. Hiding the exit is not."
     Wide, 3 s: a doorway half blocked by stacked cardboard boxes, slow push in.

SHORT 04 - The Man Who Invented the Ponzi Scheme (keep these warm and old-looking: wood, paper, no phones)
  1. "Hand him a hundred dollars, get one fifty back."
     Top-down, 3 s: hand paper-clips a few banknotes (any currency, numbers out of focus) to a handwritten note.
  2. "His secret, he claimed, was postal coupons bought cheaply in Europe."
     Top-down, 3 s: a stamp or small paper coupon placed on an old paper map or atlas page.
  3. "So why had so few coupons ever been cashed?"
     Close, 3 s: an empty wooden drawer or box pulled open under a desk lamp.
  4. "Because early investors were paid with newer investors' money."
     Close, 3 s: one hand passes a sealed envelope across a table to another hand.
  5. "Roughly fifteen million dollars poured in within about eight months."
     Top-down, 3 s: a biscuit tin or cash box being stuffed with folded notes and envelopes.
  6. "Fifty percent in forty-five days was never profit. Just someone else's deposit."
     Close, 3 s: hand drops a banknote into another open palm.

SHORT 05 - The Fake Countdown Question
  1. "Timer hit zero. The sale didn't end. Why?"
     Close, 3 s: a kitchen egg timer ticks onto zero.
  2. "So what was it counting down to?"
     Close, 3 s: phone face down on the counter, a hand hovers then pulls back.
  3. "But how often are these clocks honest?"
     Close, 3 s: three clocks or watches on a table each set to a different time.
  4. "On some, the offer stayed valid after the timer ran out."
     Close, 3 s: egg timer at zero beside a card resting on the counter (number covered).
  5. "So how do you tell? Let it run out, or return tomorrow."
     Close, 3 s: a cup of tea steaming on the table, hand turns a calendar page to the next day.
  6. "A timer that hits zero and changes nothing was never a deadline."
     Close, 3 s: egg timer needle resting on zero, slow push in.

SHORT 06 - Why 50 Percent Off Feels Irresistible
  1. "You saved fifty pounds. You're fifty pounds poorer."
     Close, 3 s: a paper shopping bag set down on the hallway floor; then a wallet opened to a few coins.
  2. "The tag said one hundred, crossed out. Now fifty."
     Close, 3 s: a handwritten paper tag on a jacket sleeve; a pen strikes through the first price.
  3. "Watch. Psychologists spun a wheel, then asked what share of UN nations were African."
     Top-down, 3 s: a hand spins a homemade paper wheel or a coin on the table.
  4. "A meaningless number dragged their judgement. That's anchoring."
     Close, 3 s: a crumpled scrap of paper with a number on it beside a key ring or small weight.
  5. "So who chose the jacket's first number? The seller."
     Close, 3 s: jackets on hangers on a door or rail, a hand pins a paper tag on one.
  6. "Weren't shopping for a jacket today? Then you didn't save fifty. You spent it."
     Close, 3 s: a card receipt held between two fingers (text blurred), then dropped on the bag.

SHORT 07 - The Price You Never Notice
  1. "Thirty pounds on screen. Forty-one at checkout."
     Close, 3 s: card tapped on the table edge as if paying (phone face down beside it).
  2. "That's drip pricing. So is it even allowed?"
     Macro, 3 s: the kitchen tap dripping into the sink.
  3. "The first fine, in twenty twenty-six, was over a three-pound booking fee."
     Top-down, 3 s: a magnifying glass over a single coin on a sheet of paper.
  4. "How big? Four point two million pounds."
     Top-down, 3 s: hands stack banknotes (or play money) under a desk lamp.
  5. "Untick what you did not choose. Compare totals, not headline prices."
     Top-down, 3 s: two printed receipts side by side, a red pen underlines each total.
  6. "Because thirty pounds was never the price. Forty-one was."
     Close, 3 s: a paper price tag tied with string to a brown parcel, hand turns it over.

SHORT 08 - The Free Gift That Costs You
  1. "Students got a free Coke. They paid double."
     Close, 3 s: an unbranded glass bottle placed on a dark table; then two coins drop into a tin.
  2. "Then his helper asked them to buy raffle tickets."
     Close, 3 s: a hand holds out a strip of raffle or cloakroom tickets across a table.
  3. "They bought about twice as many tickets. Liking him barely mattered."
     Top-down, 3 s: a pen makes tally marks in two columns on paper.
  4. "Shop samples work the same way. But why?"
     Top-down, 3 s: a few small paper cups with crisps or biscuits on a tray.
  5. "A gift you never asked for still feels like a debt."
     Close, 3 s: a small gift box tied with string set on a doormat; then a handwritten "IOU" note.
  6. "Judge the request alone, and a free Coke stays free."
     Close, 3 s: a hand pushes the tickets back across the table.

SHORT 09 - The Status Trap
  1. "Hidden under your sleeve, forever. Still worth it?"
     Close, 3 s: a coat sleeve slides down over a wristwatch.
  2. "A twenty-pound watch tells exactly the same time."
     Top-down, 3 s: two watches side by side on the table, showing the same time.
  3. "So what is the other seven hundred and eighty buying?"
     Close, 3 s: a magnifying glass over a paper price tag (numbers blurred).
  4. "In eighteen ninety-nine, economist Thorstein Veblen gave it a name."
     Close, 3 s: an old hardback book opened under a lamp, pages turning (text unreadable).
  5. "So how do you catch yourself? Try this."
     Close, 3 s: a hand holds a glossy shopping bag over the hallway table, logo turned away.
  6. "Strip the logo off in your head. Still want it? Buy it."
     Macro, 3 s: fingers peel a label off a jacket or bag.
  7. "If not, ask who the watch was really for."
     Close, 3 s: a watch left alone on a bedside table, slow push in.

SHORT 10 - Why Ordinary People Obey (keep it old-looking: wood, paper, no phones, until shot 5)
  1. "Two in three kept pressing the deadliest switch."
     Close, 3 s: a finger flicks a row of light switches (or an old power strip) one by one.
  2. "The man in the next room pounded on the wall, then went silent."
     Close, 3 s: a closed door in a bare hallway, a flat hand pressed against the wall beside it.
  3. "So why didn't they stop?"
     Close, 3 s: a hand frozen above a switch, shadows only.
  4. "A calm man in a grey lab coat simply told them to continue."
     Close, 3 s: a grey or white coat hanging on a hook; a clipboard and pen on the table.
  5. "Scammers borrow that authority, posing as banks or police."
     Close, 3 s: a phone ringing face down on a dark table, vibrating.
  6. "Two in three obeyed a lab coat. Check before you obey a caller."
     Close, 3 s: a hand turns a ringing phone face down and slides it away.

SHORT 11 - The 24 Hour Buying Rule
  1. "Eleven at night. Will you want it tomorrow?"
     Close, 3 s: a phone lit on a dark bedside table beside a glowing alarm clock.
  2. "UK law already lets you cancel most online orders."
     Top-down, 3 s: a brown parcel with a returns slip on the table (text blurred).
  3. "So why does your own brain get no cooling-off at all?"
     Close, 3 s: a crumpled receipt on a bedside table at night, lit only by a lamp (text unreadable).
  4. "Sleep on it. Twenty-four hours, no peeking."
     Wide, 3 s: a dark bedroom, a hand switches off the bedside lamp.
  5. "Why would one night change anything?"
     Close, 4 s: a sand timer (or egg timer) running out on the desk.
  6. "A pause gives the morning you a vote."
     Close, 3 s: a cup of coffee steaming on a sunny kitchen table.
  7. "So at eleven tonight, let the button wait until breakfast."
     Close, 3 s: phone face down on the nightstand beside a glass of water; then toast on a plate.

SHORT 12 - The Monthly Payment Illusion
  1. "Fifty pounds or six hundred? Same price."
     Top-down, 3 s: a single banknote placed on a wooden table, then a fat stack placed beside it.
  2. "Fifty a month, twelve months: six hundred."
     Top-down, 4 s: twelve envelopes laid in a row, one per month.
  3. "Before a single fee. How high can it go?"
     Close, 3 s: a paper price tag tied to a stack of coins.
  4. "Picture a plan that adds a twenty-pound arrangement fee."
     Top-down, 3 s: a red pen circles one line on a printed letter (text blurred).
  5. "Splitting costs can genuinely help budgeting. So where's the risk?"
     Top-down, 3 s: hands write a budget in a notebook (writing unreadable).
  6. "It shrinks the number your brain actually weighs."
     Macro, 3 s: a magnifying glass moves over a small figure on a document, then pulls back.
  7. "Before you sign, multiply. Payment times months, plus every fee."
     Top-down, 3 s: a pencil writes a sum on the back of an envelope (figures unreadable).

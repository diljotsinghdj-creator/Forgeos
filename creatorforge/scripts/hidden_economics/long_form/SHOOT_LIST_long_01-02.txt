PHONE SHOOT LIST - LONG FORM 01 and 02
Simple faceless shots to film at home on a phone. Each one replaces the picture(s) under the spoken line named.
Film in landscape (16:9), steady (prop the phone on books or a mug), daylight from a window, no faces, no readable
names, card numbers or account details (cover or blur them). Hold every shot about 3 s unless noted.

LONG 01 - Why Companies Make Cancelling So Difficult

1. "One of them could be leaving your account tonight."
   Bedside table at night, only a lamp or phone torch on: a wallet lying open, a bank card sticking out.
   Close, slightly from above, slow push in. 3 s.

2. "One tap and a card number. Ten seconds."
   Hand sliding a bank card out of a wallet on a table (card turned face down or numbers covered with tape).
   Close, side-on at table height. 3 s.

3. "On day eight, ten pounds quietly left Sam's account."
   Paper desk calendar or wall calendar: a pencil circles the 8th. Top-down, close on the date. 3 s.

4. "The catch? Every renewal arrives without an advert, sale or decision."
   A phone lying face down on a kitchen table while a printed receipt slides in beside it. Top-down. 3 s.

5. "Possible tricks are evidence, not a verdict."
   A plain brown envelope placed on a desk, a hand pressing it flat. Top-down, close. 3 s.

6. "How? Sam sets aside ten minutes and opens three months of statements."
   Kitchen timer (or an egg timer by the kettle) turned to ten minutes beside three folded sheets of paper.
   Top-down, close on the timer, then the hand sets it. 3 s.

7. "And when does it next renew?"
   A wall calendar: a red pen circles one date. Close, straight on, the pen enters from the side. 3 s.

8. "The result? Two forgotten subscriptions go."
   Scissors cutting an old, expired card in half over a table (use a loyalty card or an expired card, numbers
   covered). Close, side-on. 3 s.

LONG 02 - The Discount That Changes Your Brain Before You Buy

1. "The most persuasive price is never paid."
   A handwritten paper price tag on a jacket collar: "£100" crossed out in red marker (write it yourself, it is
   fictional). Close, slight angle, slow push in. 3 s.

2. "She is fictional, and it's Saturday night."
   Coffee table at night: a takeaway box and a glass, lamp on, laptop lid half open (screen not visible).
   Wide-ish, from behind the sofa at seat height. 3 s.

3. "It feels like forty pounds saved."
   Two notes or a few coins being slid back into a small purse on a table. Top-down, close. 3 s.

4. "It could only stop on ten or sixty-five."
   Two handwritten index cards, "10" and "65", placed side by side on a plain table (cards blurred or slightly
   out of focus if you want the number hidden). Top-down. 3 s.

5. "The partner? A clock."
   An old watch or a kitchen timer in an open palm, ticking. Close, straight down. 3 s.

6. "Back on Leah's screen, a timer appears. Offer ends in nine minutes."
   Kitchen oven timer or phone stopwatch counting down (digits soft focus). Close, straight on. 3 s.

7. "But this evening, she planned to spend nothing."
   A closed wallet resting on a coffee table next to a blank notepad and pencil. Top-down. 3 s.

8. "Leah asked it, and closed the laptop."
   A hand slowly closing a laptop lid on a sofa cushion. Side-on, low angle. 3 s.

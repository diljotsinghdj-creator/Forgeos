In nineteen seventy-four, two psychologists rigged a wheel of fortune.

It looked random, but it only ever stopped on ten or sixty-five.

Then they asked a question that had nothing to do with the wheel.

What share of the United Nations' members are African countries?

People who saw ten gave a median guess of twenty-five percent.

Psychologists call this anchoring. The first number becomes a starting point.

Around three decades later, researchers tried it with prices.

Finally, they asked each student for the most they would pay.

Students with the highest numbers bid far more than those with the lowest.

The crossed-out price is the anchor. The sale price is the adjustment.

And here is the uncomfortable part. The shop chooses the anchor.

Stock: pricing clothes in shop

Many reductions are genuine, and a real discount is useful.

European lawmakers took this seriously enough to write it into law.

Stock: European Parliament building

Since May twenty twenty-two, an advertised reduction across the EU must show a specific reference price.
Visual: Close-up of a printed legal text on a desk, an official seal visible, words blurred

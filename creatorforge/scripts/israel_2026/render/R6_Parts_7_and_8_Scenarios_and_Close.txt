And for Palestinians, the roads look different from how they look in Jerusalem. A Netanyahu government with Ben-Gvir and Smotrich would likely continue its push in the West Bank, given what its ministers say they want. An Eisenkot government says it would not annex Gaza and criticised E1, but it also rejects a Palestinian state and promises to keep fighting Hamas. Neither offers what Palestinian leaders ask for: an end to occupation and a state.

Chapter: Part 8 | What It Settled

So did this election settle anything? It did, actually. A few things.
Tone: calm
Visual: The Knesset building in Jerusalem at dawn
Visual: A pen resting on a closed notebook

It settled that Israelis are still deeply engaged in their democracy. Almost four in five voted. Arab citizens voted in historic numbers. That matters, and it should be said without a punchline.
Tone: calm
Visual: Voters seen from behind queueing outside a polling station in morning light
Visual: A ballot slip falling into a blue box
Text: TURNOUT ~78% · HIGHEST SINCE 1999

It settled that the country is split almost exactly down the middle on Netanyahu, and that the middle itself is mostly defined by who can sit with whom.

Visual: Two halves of an apple on a cutting board

And it settled, quietly, that the two men who want to lead Israel agree on more than their campaigns let on: no Palestinian state, a long military presence, and a war with Hamas that isn't over until Hamas is.

What it did not settle: who governs, who pays for the war, who answers for October seventh, whether Haredi men serve, and what happens to the millions of people who live under Israel's control and have no vote at all.

# The Vox explainer method - study for Print Facts / Dragon Psychology

Sources: interviews with Vox producers (Joss Fong, Estelle Caswell, Phil Edwards, the Borders team), Nieman Lab and
Digiday reporting, and third-party style breakdowns (marked *breakdown*). Vox has no public style guide; copy the
**method and craft**, never Vox's name, logo, typeface, music or footage.

## 1. The story comes first (what Vox says matters most)
- **One question per video**, answered in 3-5 beats. Cold open on a concrete, surprising image or fact - not an intro.
- **Visual evidence first.** A story editor checks every script line asks "what is on screen proving this?" (Caswell:
  "video is about talking about the thing onscreen"). The review takes 4-5 days at Vox. No line without evidence.
- **Pick stories that work better as video than text** (Fong) - things you can *show*: maps, data, documents, archive.
- **Build up**: start from basics, add one idea at a time, one central visual thread (Earworm).
- **Return to the opening image** at the end with the answer (*breakdown*).
- **Data-heavy**: Fong's gun-violence video = a week of UN data, 18 charts. Numbers are shown, not just said.
- **Make the trivial non-trivial** (Phil Edwards): an ordinary object/topic with a surprising history.
- **One person owns a video** pitch → publication; sometimes the host appears on camera briefly (Edwards).
- **Series**, not one-offs: Borders, Earworm, Atlas, Missing Chapter - recurring formats viewers return to.

## 2. The look
- **Paper**: off-white/cream textured background, kept the whole video.
- **Palette**: muted beige / black / grey + **one accent** (yellow; red for warnings). Flat colour, no gradients.
- **Archive photos as paper cut-outs**: white edge, soft shadow, halftone dots, tape/torn edges - *real* photos.
- **Documents and articles**: the real page, camera **pushes in slowly** (grain, slight focus shift), a **yellow
  highlighter sweeps the phrase as it is spoken**; sometimes a red pen circle.
- **Kinetic type**: big, bold, condensed words appear *as they are spoken*, centre frame, not lower thirds.
- **Maps**: label-free muted base map; routes draw themselves; camera flies in and orbits; **photos and documents
  pinned on the map like evidence** (drop shadows); historical maps cross-fade fast (< 1 s each) (*breakdown*).
- **Charts**: minimal, flat, one accent, animate in sync with the number being said; many small charts > one busy one.
- **Footage**: archival clips framed (TV/border), grainy, never full-bleed glossy stock.

## 3. Motion and edit
- **12 fps "stutter"** on graphics inside a 24/30 fps timeline - the hand-made feel (*breakdown*).
- Elements **slide, drop or snap** into place - they don't glide (*breakdown*).
- **Camera-blur transitions**: a fast blurred slide/whip between graphics (*breakdown*).
- **Every change is timed to a word** of narration; new visual every 2-4 s; a beat = 1-3 sentences, 8-14 s.
- **Pacing matches the music**: how long a graphic stays matches the beat of the score (Fong, Open Notebook).

## 4. Sound
- Calm, conversational, curious narration (Fong admits it can feel "awkward and cheesy" - they keep it plain).
- Licensed production music (APM at Vox) - quiet, rhythmic; music dips under key lines.
- Small foley: paper, pops, whooshes on moves, typing on text - low under the voice.

## 5. Business (how Vox grew on YouTube)
- YouTube-first, ~5 videos/week at the start, sub-series per topic, "smart content for a curious audience".
- Built audience first, monetised later; a **YouTube membership** ("Video Lab") with behind-the-scenes extras.

## 7. Rules for every Print Facts script (adopted from the above)
1. One question; cold open on the most surprising concrete fact.
2. Every line gets one piece of on-screen evidence (article, document, quote, chart, map, real clip) - no AI picture.
3. A new graphic every 2-4 s; no graphic type twice in a row.
4. Numbers always as charts; documents always highlighted on the spoken phrase.
5. Show the other side on screen ("दूसरा पक्ष"), label allegations ("आरोप").
6. End by returning to the opening image with the answer.
